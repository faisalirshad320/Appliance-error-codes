GE refrigerator error codes: what the display is telling you

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · GE refrigerator

What it means

GE refrigerators show faults as a short two-letter code in the temperature display, and the letter pair tells you which system is complaining. PF means the power was interrupted, FF warns that the freezer has warmed up, CC means the cabinet is not holding the temperature you set, CI points at the ice maker, and dF or dE mean the defrost system has not run when it should have. PF is the one most people see and it clears with a button press. The ones that do not clear lead toward the defrost system or the sealed refrigerant circuit, and the sealed system is licensed-technician work with no DIY path at all.

Safety first

A refrigerator's sealed refrigerant circuit, meaning the compressor, the condenser and evaporator coils and every pipe between them, is a licensed-technician job in the United States and is not something to attempt whatever a video shows you. Opening it vents refrigerant, which is both illegal to release and a real injury risk, and recharging it requires certification and equipment you do not have. Unplug the refrigerator at the wall before removing any panel, and never bypass a thermal cut-out or a door switch.

Why a GE refrigerator shows this code

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Power interruption, shown as PFmost casesThe code appeared after a storm, an outage or someone resetting a breaker, and the fridge is otherwise running normally and cold. This is a notice rather than a fault. It exists to tell you the cabinet may have warmed while you were out, so you can judge the food rather than guess.
  2. Temperature not being held, shown as FF or CCcommonFF warns the freezer has risen enough to worry about frozen food; CC says the compartment is not reaching the setpoint. Put a thermometer in a glass of water in the fridge and leave it four hours. Before blaming the machine, check the door seals, an overfilled cabinet blocking the vents, and whether the condenser coils underneath are matted with dust.
  3. Defrost system fault, shown as dF or dEcommonThe control has not seen the defrost circuit run within the period it expects. The symptom owners notice first is a freezer that still works while the fridge section slowly warms, and a thick sheet of frost building on the back wall inside the freezer. The heater, the thermostat or the sensor is the usual cause, and this is a repair, not a reset.
  4. Ice maker fault, shown as CIcommonNo ice, or hollow and undersized cubes, alongside the code. Check the fill tube for an ice plug, the water supply valve behind the fridge, and whether the ice maker arm has been knocked into the off position. A kinked supply line behind a fridge that was recently pushed back is a classic.
  5. Evaporator or condenser fan failureoccasionalThe fridge runs constantly but neither side gets properly cold, and the freezer is colder than the fridge. Open the freezer and listen: the evaporator fan should start within a few seconds. Silence, or a rattling and grinding noise, points at the fan or at ice jamming the blade.
  6. Control board or communication faultoccasionalThe display shows nonsense, flickers between codes, or the controls do not respond while the compressor behaves normally. A five-minute power cycle clears a genuine one-off. A code that returns immediately after a correct reset usually means a board or a harness problem rather than the system the code names.
  7. Compressor or sealed refrigerant circuit failurerareNothing anywhere in the cabinet is getting cold, the compressor is either silent or clicking on and off every few minutes, and the condenser coils are clean. This is the one branch of the list with no DIY path. Stop here and call a licensed technician.

Fix it step by step

  1. Write the code down exactly as shown

    Photograph the display. On a seven-segment panel a letter O and a zero look nearly identical, and dF and dE are a single segment apart while meaning different work. Note whether the code sits in the fridge display or the freezer display, because on several GE models that is what tells you which compartment the fault belongs to.

    3 min

  2. Check whether it is a notice or a fault

    PF is a power failure notice, not a breakdown, and on most models it clears by pressing any temperature adjustment button or the designated reset key on the panel. Do that first. If the fridge is cold, running normally and the only oddity is PF, you have nothing to fix beyond deciding whether the food in the freezer is still good.

    5 min

  3. Measure the actual temperatures before trusting the display

    Stand a glass of water in the fridge with a thermometer in it and leave it four hours, and put a thermometer between frozen packages in the freezer. This separates a real cooling fault from a sensor or display fault, which is the single most useful thing you can establish before anyone comes out. Note both readings down; a technician will ask.

    240 minTools: Two thermometers

  4. Clean the condenser coils and check airflow

    Unplug the fridge. Pull it out and take the rear access panel or the front kick grille off, depending on the model. Vacuum the coils and the fan area with a crevice tool and a soft brush, working gently so you do not bend the fins. Inside the cabinet, check nothing is packed against the vents at the back of either compartment, and check the door seals grip a sheet of paper all the way round.

    40 minTools: Vacuum with crevice tool, soft brush, Phillips screwdriver

    Unplug at the wall first. Do not pull, bend or pierce any copper pipework behind the coils; that is the sealed refrigerant circuit.

  5. Do a full five-minute power reset

    Unplug the refrigerator at the wall or switch off its breaker, wait five full minutes, then restore power. The board holds residual charge and a quick off-and-on often leaves the stored code in place. Leave the doors shut and give it a few hours before judging whether it worked, because a fridge that has been reset has to pull the whole cabinet back down to temperature.

    10 min

  6. Check the ice maker side if the code points there

    Pull the ice bin out and look at the fill tube at the back of the ice maker for a plug of ice, which you can thaw with a cloth soaked in warm water. Check the feeler arm is down rather than lifted into the off position. Behind the fridge, confirm the water supply valve is open and the line is not kinked where the fridge was pushed back against it.

    20 minTools: Cloth, warm water

  7. Stop and get help if the code points at cooling

    If your thermometers confirm the cabinet is genuinely not cold, the coils are clean, the seals are sound and a reset has not helped, this is where the DIY path ends. The remaining causes are the defrost system, the fans, the control board and the sealed refrigerant circuit, and the last of those needs certification. Move the food somewhere cold and book a technician rather than running the machine and hoping.

    15 min

    Do not attempt any repair involving the compressor, the refrigerant lines or recharging the system. That work is restricted to licensed technicians.

How to reset it

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall socket or switch off its breaker, wait a full five minutes, then restore power and leave the doors closed. The wait matters because the control board holds charge and a quick off-and-on frequently leaves the stored code in place. Give it several hours before you judge the result, since the cabinet has to pull back down to temperature from wherever the interruption left it. A PF notice is different and usually clears with a button press on the panel rather than needing an unplug. Be aware that pulling the power also erases stored fault history, which a technician may have wanted, so photograph the code first.

It still shows the code

If the reset has not cleared it and the coils are clean, the next move depends entirely on what your thermometers told you, so do that measurement before anything else. If both compartments are at the right temperature and the only problem is a code on the display, you most likely have a sensor or a board fault rather than a cooling fault, and the food is safe while you arrange a visit. That is the cheapest branch: a thermistor is a modest part and a reasonable repair. If the freezer is cold but the fridge is warming, and you can see a thick even sheet of frost on the rear wall inside the freezer, that is the classic defrost failure pattern and it points at the defrost heater, the defrost thermostat or the defrost sensor. Expect $250 to $450 fitted, and expect it to be a genuine repair rather than a reset. If neither compartment is cold, listen at the back: a compressor that runs steadily with clean coils and no cooling, or one that clicks on and off every few minutes, means the sealed refrigerant circuit, and that is where this stops being yours. Sealed system work runs $600 to $1,500 and on a refrigerator over about ten years old it often costs more than the machine is worth, so get a written diagnosis and a quote before you authorize anything. One thing worth doing immediately rather than waiting: if the cabinet is above 40 degrees Fahrenheit and has been for more than a few hours, move the food. The code is a problem for next week; spoiled food is a problem today. And if the display is cycling through several different codes in succession rather than holding one, treat that as a board or harness fault regardless of what the individual codes name, because a board that cannot read its sensors will report faults in systems that are perfectly healthy.

Differences between models

GE has used more than one code scheme across its refrigerator range and the overlap is imperfect. The two-letter codes described here, PF, FF, CC, CI, dF and dE, are the family most owners meet on side-by-side and French-door models with a temperature display. GE Profile, Cafe and Monogram models carry additional codes and a more detailed service diagnostic mode, and Monogram in particular uses a different set again. Several published button sequences for entering diagnostic mode, often described as holding the two temperature buttons together for several seconds, differ between series, and using the wrong one does nothing. Code tables that show numeric codes such as 3E or 4E are generally written for a different manufacturer's scheme and do not apply to GE. Use the owner's manual or the service data sheet for your exact model number, and when a generic chart disagrees with it, believe the manual.

When to stop and call someone

Call a licensed technician for anything that touches the compressor, the refrigerant lines or recharging the sealed system, with no exceptions and regardless of what you find online. That work needs certification both for safety and because venting refrigerant is illegal, and there is no partial version of it you can do yourself. Beyond that, call once you have confirmed with a thermometer that the cabinet genuinely is not holding temperature, the condenser coils are clean, the door seals are sound and a five-minute reset has not helped, because the remaining causes all require the machine stripped and tested live. Call immediately rather than waiting if the compressor is clicking on and off every few minutes, if you can smell anything burning, or if there is oily residue on the pipework or the floor beneath the fridge, which means refrigerant has escaped. Move the food somewhere cold first; that is more urgent than the booking.

Questions people ask

What does PF mean on a GE refrigerator?

PF means the power to the refrigerator was interrupted, by an outage, a tripped breaker or someone unplugging it. It is a notice rather than a fault, and it exists so you can judge whether the freezer contents warmed while you were out. On most models it clears by pressing a temperature adjustment button on the panel. Nothing needs repairing.

What is the difference between dF and dE on a GE fridge?

Both point at the defrost system, which is the circuit that periodically melts frost off the evaporator coil. The published sources differ on the exact timing window each one represents, but the practical meaning is the same: the control has not seen defrost run when it should have. Either one is a real repair involving the heater, thermostat or sensor, not something a reset fixes.

Can I fix a GE refrigerator sealed system myself?

No. The sealed system covers the compressor, the coils and every refrigerant line between them, and working on it requires certification in the United States. Venting refrigerant is illegal and genuinely hazardous, and recharging needs equipment you will not have. If a diagnosis points there, the only question left is whether to repair or replace the appliance.

My GE fridge shows a code but everything is still cold. Is that urgent?

Less urgent than it looks, but do not ignore it. Confirm it with thermometers in both compartments over four hours rather than trusting the display. If both are at temperature, you probably have a sensor or board fault, your food is safe, and you can book a visit at your convenience. If either is warm, treat it as urgent and move the food.

How do I get into GE refrigerator diagnostic mode?

There is no single sequence that works across the range. Published instructions usually involve holding two temperature buttons together for several seconds, but the exact combination differs between GE, Profile, Cafe and Monogram series, and the wrong one does nothing. Look it up for your specific model number, and photograph any code first because unplugging the fridge erases stored faults.

Why did my GE fridge start showing a code suddenly?

The most common trigger is a power event, which is exactly what PF reports. The other common one is gradual: condenser coils matting with dust over a couple of years until the machine can no longer hold temperature on a warm day, at which point a temperature code appears and looks sudden. Vacuuming the coils twice a year prevents a surprising number of these.

Is it worth repairing a GE refrigerator that throws a cooling code?

It depends which repair. A sensor or a fan is modest and worth doing on almost any machine. A defrost system repair at $250 to $450 is usually worth it under ten years old. Sealed system work at $600 to $1,500 rarely is on an older fridge. Get a written diagnosis and quote before authorizing anything.
