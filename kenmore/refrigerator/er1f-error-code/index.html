Kenmore refrigerator Er 1F error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Kenmore refrigerator

What it means

Er 1F means the control cannot see the ice maker fan turning. That is the small fan that moves cold air through the ice compartment, and when it stops, ice production slows or stops while the rest of the fridge carries on looking fine. The usual cause is frost building up around the blade until it jams, not a dead motor. Worth knowing before you start: the characters are Er IF, with a capital I for ice, and the display draws that I as a plain vertical bar, which is why everybody reads it as the number one.

Safety first

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall before removing any interior panel. Never chip or pry ice away with a screwdriver, a knife or anything else sharp near the cooling coils: they are thin tubing under refrigerant pressure, and puncturing one turns a cheap fan repair into a sealed-system job. Sealed refrigerant work is licensed-technician work and must not be attempted at home. Warm water, towels and patience are the only correct tools for ice.

Why a Kenmore refrigerator shows ER1F

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Frost has built up around the ice maker fanmost casesGet the ice compartment cover off with the fridge unplugged. A fan blade furred with frost, or one you cannot turn with a finger, settles it. This is the common outcome and it needs time rather than parts.
  2. A door or the ice compartment flap is not sealingmost casesClose each door onto a dollar bill at several points along every edge; it should drag everywhere. On French-door models check that the ice compartment door inside the fresh food door is latching and that nothing in the door bins is holding it open. Humid air getting in is what grows the frost in the first place.
  3. The ice maker fan motor has failedcommonThe frost is gone, the blade spins freely and coasts when you flick it, and the fan still does nothing when the fridge is running with the door switch held in. A motor that is seized will not turn by hand at all; one that has failed electrically turns perfectly and never starts.
  4. A loose connector or broken wire at the fanoccasionalBlade clear and free, but the connector is slack in your fingers, shows green corrosion, or has a pin pushed back into its housing. Check the harness where it crosses into the compartment, because the wires flex there and can break inside the insulation while looking perfect.
  5. The defrost system is not clearing the coiloccasionalYou defrosted it, it ran fine for a week or two, and Er 1F came back. The fan is a symptom. Something in the defrost heater, the defrost thermostat or the control is failing to clear frost on schedule, so it rebuilds until the blade jams again.
  6. The main control boardrareA new fan motor, a clean connector and a defrosted compartment, and the code still appears. This is the last answer, not the first, and it is worth a second opinion before paying for a board.

Fix it step by step

  1. Confirm who actually built your refrigerator

    Kenmore is a badge rather than a factory, and this code comes from the LG-built units. Find the model number on the sticker inside the fresh food compartment, usually on the left wall or behind a crisper. The first three digits identify the manufacturer, and the LG-built Kenmore refrigerators start 795. If yours starts with something else, the code format on your machine is different and this page is not the right one.

    5 min

  2. Check the doors and the ice compartment seal

    Close each door onto a dollar bill at the top, middle and bottom of every edge and pull; it should drag at every point. Then open the fresh food door and check the ice compartment door inside it closes and latches fully, with nothing in the door bins fouling it. A compartment flap held open a quarter inch will frost a fan solid within a fortnight.

    10 min

  3. Listen for the fan with the door switch held in

    Find the door switch plunger in the door frame and press and hold it so the fridge behaves as though the door is shut. Listen at the ice compartment. You are listening for a small fan spinning up within a few seconds. Silence tells you one thing, a buzz with no airflow another, and a rattling or grinding noise says the blade is hitting ice.

    5 min

  4. Unplug and open the ice compartment

    Pull the plug at the wall. Take out the ice bin and any shelves in the way, then remove the screws holding the ice compartment cover, usually two to four Phillips screws with one often hidden behind a plastic plug. Ease the cover off rather than levering it; if it is frozen in place, lay a cloth soaked in hot water against it for a few minutes and try again.

    20 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, hot water, cloths

    Unplug at the wall first. Do not force a frozen cover; a cracked cover is a part you then have to buy.

  5. Defrost it thoroughly rather than quickly

    Leave the refrigerator unplugged with the doors open and towels along the base. Eight hours clears most of it and a full day is better if the compartment had iced up badly. A hairdryer on low, held well back and kept moving, speeds things up without heating any one spot. Never use a heat gun, a steamer, or anything you have to push into the ice.

    480 minTools: Towels, hairdryer

    Nothing sharp near the coils or tubing. A punctured line means a licensed sealed-system repair, not a fan swap.

  6. Spin the blade and inspect the connector

    Still unplugged. Turn the fan blade with a fingertip: it should spin freely and coast briefly. Grinding, catching, or refusing to move means a seized motor. Then pull the fan connector, look for corrosion or a pin backed out of the housing, push it home until it clicks, and run your fingers along the harness where it passes into the compartment feeling for a break under the insulation.

    10 minTools: Flashlight

    Refrigerator unplugged while you have hands behind the panel.

  7. Reassemble and watch it for a fortnight

    Refit the cover and the bin, plug the fridge back in, and give it a few hours before expecting ice. Then keep an eye on it. A code that clears after a defrost and returns two weeks later has not been fixed: the frost is rebuilding, and the fault is in the defrost circuit rather than in the fan you just thawed.

    20 minTools: Phillips screwdriver

How to reset it

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall, or switch its breaker off, for a full minute, then restore power and give the control a couple of minutes to initialise. The reset clears the code from the display but changes nothing about an iced or failed fan, so a code that returns within a day is reporting a real fault. Use the reset after the repair to confirm it has actually cleared rather than being remembered.

It still shows ER1F

If a proper defrost cleared it and it came back within a few weeks, the fan was never the fault. Something is failing to clear frost on schedule, which puts you in the defrost circuit: the heater, the defrost thermostat, or the control that runs them. A technician can check all three in one visit, and that is a far better use of money than a second fan motor and another day of defrosting. If the defrost did not clear it at all, work up in cost. First, with the fridge unplugged, flick the blade. Free and coasting means the motor is not seized and the fault is electrical; grinding or solid means the motor is. Second, before ordering anything, pull the connector and look at it under a torch. Corrosion and pins backed out of their housings are common in a compartment that gets damp on every defrost cycle, and both cost nothing to put right. Squeeze along the harness where it crosses into the compartment, because a wire broken inside intact insulation looks perfect and tests dead. Third, if the blade is free, the wiring is sound and the fan still never runs, fit a new fan motor: it is a $40 to $140 part and usually one connector plus a clipped or screwed mount. Fourth, only if a new motor changes nothing does the board become the answer, and that is several hundred dollars on a repair that began as a free defrost. One practical note while you wait: with the ice maker fan out of action the rest of the refrigerator generally holds temperature normally, so this is an inconvenience rather than a food-safety emergency, unlike a main evaporator fan failure. If your fresh food compartment is also warming up, you have a second problem and it needs looking at first.

Differences between models

Two things about this code cause more confusion than the fault itself. First, the characters are Er IF with a capital I for ice maker, and on these displays a capital I is drawn as a single vertical bar that is indistinguishable from the number one, which is why it is searched and written as Er 1F at least as often. They are the same code. Second, Kenmore does not manufacture refrigerators, so the code set depends on who built yours: this format belongs to the LG-built machines, whose model numbers begin 795, and the identical code appears on LG-badged refrigerators. Those machines use a small family of fan codes, with separate ones for the freezer fan and the fresh food fan, so check which letters you actually have before ordering a part.

When to stop and call someone

Call a technician if the code returns within a few weeks of a thorough defrost, because the fault is in the defrost circuit and diagnosing it needs the coil exposed and a meter on the heater and thermostat. Call as well if the fresh food or freezer compartment is also failing to hold temperature, since that is a different and more urgent fault than a stopped ice maker fan. Any mention of refrigerant, a recharge or a sealed-system repair means a licensed technician, with no exceptions and no home attempts. Stop and call if you find oily residue on or around any of the tubing, which indicates a leak rather than a fan problem.

Questions people ask

Is Er 1F the same as Er IF?

Yes, they are one code. The characters are Er IF, where the I stands for the ice maker fan, and the refrigerator's display draws a capital I as a plain vertical bar that looks exactly like a one. There is no separate Er 1F fault. Whichever way you read it off the panel, the control is reporting that the ice maker fan is not turning.

Can I still use my refrigerator with an Er 1F code?

Usually yes. This fan serves the ice compartment, so what you lose is ice production rather than cooling, and the rest of the fridge generally holds temperature normally. Treat it as an inconvenience with a deadline rather than an emergency. If the fresh food side is also warming up, that is a separate and more urgent fault.

Why does my Kenmore show an LG error code?

Because LG built it. Kenmore is a badge that Sears applied to appliances made by several manufacturers, and the LG-built refrigerators carry LG's control platform and its code format. Check the model number inside the fresh food compartment: the LG-built units begin 795. The repair, the parts and the diagnosis are all the LG ones.

Will defrosting fix an Er 1F code?

In most cases, yes, because frost jamming the fan blade is the usual cause. Unplug the fridge, empty the ice compartment, and leave the doors open for eight to twenty-four hours with towels down. What defrosting will not do is stop it happening again, so find out why humid air was getting in before you reload the freezer.

Why did the Er 1F code come back after I defrosted it?

Because the frost rebuilt, which means something is not clearing the coil on schedule. That points at the defrost heater, the defrost thermostat or the control rather than the fan. The other common reason is a door or ice compartment flap that is not sealing, letting warm humid air in continuously. Check the seals before paying for defrost parts.

How much does it cost to fix an Er 1F error?

Nothing at all if a defrost and a reseated connector do it, which is the common outcome. An ice maker fan motor is $40 to $140 in parts, or roughly $200 to $450 fitted. Defrost circuit parts are $30 to $90 plus labour. A control board runs into several hundred and should be the last thing anyone proposes.

Does Er 1F mean the refrigerator needs refrigerant?

No. The code reports a fan that is not turning, which is a mechanical or electrical fault with nothing to do with the refrigerant charge. Be cautious with anyone who quotes a recharge off the back of this code without opening the compartment. Genuine sealed-system work is licensed-technician territory and a completely different repair.
