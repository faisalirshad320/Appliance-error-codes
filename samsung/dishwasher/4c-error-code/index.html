Samsung dishwasher 4C error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Samsung dishwasher

What it means

4C means the dishwasher called for water and did not get enough of it within the time the control allows. On a dishwasher the supply is a single cold line, so there is a short list of things between your plumbing and the tub: the shutoff valve under the sink, the fill hose, the screen in the inlet fitting, and the water inlet valve behind the kickplate. Most of the time the fault is on the supply side rather than in the machine, and the shutoff valve under the sink is the first thing to look at. Older Samsung dishwashers show the same fault as 4E.

Why a Samsung dishwasher shows 4C

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. The shutoff valve under the sink is closed or only cracked openmost casesOpen the sink cabinet and find the small valve on the hot water line feeding the dishwasher. A quarter-turn lever must sit in line with the pipe, not across it; a round handle should turn counterclockwise until it stops. If anyone has worked under that sink recently, assume it was turned down and never put back.
  2. The inlet screen at the fill hose connection is cloggedmost casesClose the valve, undo the fill line where it meets the dishwasher inlet, and look into the fitting. There is a fine mesh screen set in there. Bright metal is fine; a brown, scaled or furred screen is your fault. Grit collects there after any mains work in the street or the house.
  3. The fill line is kinked or crushed behind the machinecommonPull the dishwasher forward and look at the line where it passes through the cabinet side and behind the leveling legs. A flat spot where the machine was shoved back still passes a trickle, which is why the tub fills slowly rather than not at all.
  4. House water pressure is genuinely too lowoccasionalPut the loose end of the fill line into a bucket and open the valve for five seconds. You want a strong jet that puts a few inches in the bucket. A weak stream means the restriction is upstream, in the valve body, the supply line, or your plumbing, and the dishwasher is reporting it accurately.
  5. The water inlet valve on the machine has failedoccasionalStrong flow into a bucket, clean screen, straight line, valve wide open, and still 4C. The inlet valve behind the kickplate is either electrically open circuit or mechanically stuck shut. This is the point where a part is actually needed.
  6. The supply line has frozenrareOnly in a cold snap, and only where the line runs through an unheated space such as a crawlspace, an outside wall or a vacation home left unheated. The line is rigid and very cold to the touch, and other cold fixtures on the same run are affected too.

Fix it step by step

  1. Open the shutoff valve fully

    Clear out the sink cabinet and find the valve on the line that feeds the dishwasher, usually on the hot supply beside the faucet risers. Turn a round handle counterclockwise until it stops, or swing a quarter-turn lever so it sits parallel to the pipe. Half open is the common state and it fills the tub too slowly to satisfy the control, which is exactly what 4C reports.

    5 minTools: Flashlight

  2. Cancel the cycle and power cycle the dishwasher

    Press Cancel or hold the Start button to end the program, then switch the machine off at its breaker for five minutes and back on. The control stores the fault, so a dishwasher that now has a perfectly good water supply will keep showing 4C until the stored code is cleared. Start a rinse-only program to test rather than a full wash.

    8 min

  3. Pull the machine out and inspect the fill line

    Switch off the breaker and close the shutoff valve, then undo the two mounting screws under the countertop and ease the dishwasher forward. Follow the fill line from the valve to the machine, looking for a sharp bend, a flat crushed section, or a point where it is pinched behind a leveling leg. Straighten what you find and leave clearance behind the machine when you slide it back.

    15 minTools: Phillips or Torx screwdriver, flashlight

    Close the shutoff valve and cut the breaker before moving a plumbed-in dishwasher.

  4. Clean the inlet screen

    With the valve closed, put a towel and a shallow pan under the connection and unscrew the fill line from the dishwasher inlet with an adjustable wrench. Expect a cup of water. Inside the inlet fitting is a fine mesh screen; lift it out with needle-nose pliers, rinse it under a hot tap, scrub it with an old toothbrush and press it back in squarely. Reconnect hand tight plus a quarter turn with the wrench, no more.

    15 minTools: Adjustable wrench, needle-nose pliers, old toothbrush, towel, shallow pan

    Close the shutoff valve before undoing the fill line or you take mains pressure in the face.

  5. Test the flow into a bucket

    With the fill line still disconnected at the machine, hold its loose end down in a bucket and open the shutoff valve for five seconds, then close it. A healthy supply puts a strong jet into the bucket and several inches of water with it. A limp dribble means the problem is upstream of the dishwasher, often an old multi-turn valve that has silted up internally, and the fix is in your plumbing.

    8 minTools: Bucket

  6. Check the inlet valve at the machine

    Power off at the breaker. Remove the kickplate, usually two or four screws along the bottom edge, and find the inlet valve at the bottom of the machine where the fill line enters, normally on the left. Check the electrical connector is fully seated and free of green corrosion. With a multimeter on ohms across the solenoid terminals, a working coil reads a steady resistance and a dead one reads open circuit; check the service sheet for your model for the figure it should be, rather than guessing from a number online.

    25 minTools: Screwdriver, multimeter, flashlight

    Power off at the breaker before removing the kickplate. Do not test the valve circuit live.

  7. Thaw a frozen line if the run is in an unheated space

    Close the shutoff valve and wrap the suspect section in towels soaked in hot tap water, swapping them as they cool. Give it half an hour and try again. Never put a heat gun, a torch or boiling water on a braided or plastic supply line; it will split and you will find out at full pressure. Once it is running, insulate the run before the next cold snap.

    35 minTools: Towels, hot water

    No open flame and no boiling water on a supply line, ever.

How to reset it

Press Cancel, or hold the Start button until the program ends, then switch the dishwasher off at its breaker for five minutes and back on. The five minutes matters because the control holds the stored fault and a quick off-on frequently leaves 4C on the display even after the water supply is fixed. Test with a rinse-only program so you find out within a couple of minutes rather than at the end of a long wash.

It still shows 4C

If the valve is fully open, the screen is clean, the line is straight and a bucket test gives a strong jet, you have ruled out the supply and the fault is inside the machine. Go in cost order. First, check the connector at the inlet valve: pull the kickplate with the power off and look for green corrosion or a pin backed out of the housing, which happens under dishwashers that have had a leak and costs nothing to fix. Second, measure across the valve solenoid with a multimeter; an open circuit confirms the valve is dead, and that is a $35 to $85 part and about forty minutes with the machine pulled forward. Third, if the solenoid reads correctly but never energizes when the machine calls for water, the problem is the harness or the main control board, and that is where the bill jumps toward a few hundred dollars. Two things worth ruling out before you spend anything. One: the shutoff valve itself. Old multi-turn valves silt up internally and look fully open while passing almost nothing, and a weak bucket test with a wide-open valve points straight at it; a plumber replacing it with a quarter-turn ball valve is a cheaper and more useful job than anything done to the dishwasher. Two: if 4C only appears at certain times of day, or while a washing machine or shower is running, your supply pressure is marginal rather than your appliance faulty, and the dishwasher is just the first thing in the house to complain about it.

Differences between models

Samsung shows this fault as 4C on newer dishwashers and 4E on older ones, and the meaning and the fix are the same on both. Some models display a water-tap indicator alongside or instead of the code. Dishwashers are normally plumbed to a single supply, so unlike a Samsung washer there is no hot and cold pair to confuse and no swapped-hose variant of this fault to chase.

When to stop and call someone

Call an appliance technician if the bucket test gives strong flow, the screen is clean, and the inlet valve solenoid reads correctly on a meter but never opens, because the next step is tracing a live circuit back to the board. Call a plumber instead if the bucket test is weak with the valve wide open, since the fault is in the shutoff valve or the supply line and an appliance engineer will charge a call-out to tell you the same thing. And if you find water around the inlet connection while you are in there, shut the valve and deal with that first: a leak at the supply end does far more damage than a dishwasher that will not fill.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between 4C and 4E on a Samsung dishwasher?

Nothing. They are the same water supply fault shown differently across model generations, with 4E on older machines and 4C on newer ones. Every check on this page applies to both. If your display shows a water-tap symbol rather than a code, that is the same message in a different form: the machine did not get the water it asked for.

Where is the water shutoff valve for a dishwasher?

Under the kitchen sink, almost always. Look for a small valve on the hot supply line beside the faucet risers, with a line running from it through the side of the cabinet toward the dishwasher. In some installations it is in the basement directly below, or behind an access panel. There is rarely a valve behind the dishwasher itself.

Can I still use the dishwasher with a 4C code?

No, because the machine stops rather than washing without enough water. There is no safety risk in leaving it showing the code. If the fault turns out to be marginal pressure rather than a blockage, you may find it completes a cycle when nothing else in the house is drawing water, but that is a symptom to fix rather than a workaround.

Why did 4C appear suddenly when nothing changed?

Inlet screens clog gradually and then cross a line, so the failure feels sudden after months of building. The other usual trigger is work elsewhere: a plumber turning a valve down, a water meter swap, or a street main repair that stirs sediment into your pipes and lands it in the screen within a week.

Does 4C mean the inlet valve needs replacing?

Usually not. In most cases the valve is fine and the restriction is a half-closed shutoff, a clogged screen, or a crushed fill line. Only replace the valve after a bucket test shows strong flow and a meter shows the solenoid open circuit, because a new valve on a restricted supply throws the same code on its first cycle.

The dishwasher fills but still shows 4C. Why?

It is filling too slowly rather than not at all, so the control times out before the water level is reached. That points at a partly blocked screen, a half-open shutoff valve, or low house pressure rather than a dead valve. Clean the screen and open the shutoff fully before you suspect anything electrical.
