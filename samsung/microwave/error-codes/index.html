Samsung microwave error codes: how to read them and what to do

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Samsung microwave

What it means

Samsung groups its microwave codes into families, which makes them far easier to read than they look. SE is a key short error, meaning the control sees a button stuck or held. The C codes work in threes by subject: C-d0, C-d1 and C-d2 are door or key errors, C-20 upward are temperature sensing, C-70 upward are steam sensing, C-A are functional errors and C-F are communication between boards. SE is the one most people are looking at, and the first thing worth knowing is that the display draws an S and a 5 the same way, so SE and 5E are one code.

Safety first

Do not remove the cabinet from a microwave. The high-voltage capacitor inside holds a charge that can kill you, it remains charged after the oven is unplugged, and it does not reliably discharge by itself. Every check on this page is deliberately outside the case. Never operate the oven with a panel off, never interfere with a door latch or interlock switch, and unplug it and stop using it if it arcs, smokes, smells of burning or appears to run with the door open.

Why a Samsung microwave shows this code

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A key reading as stuck or shortedmost casesThis is SE, and it is the code Samsung microwave owners see more than all the others together. Press each key separately and watch the display: the one that does nothing, fires the wrong function, or needs much harder pressure is the one the control is reading as held down.
  2. Moisture or grease behind the touch panelmost casesSE appeared after cleaning the front, after a steamy cooking job, or on an over-the-range unit sitting in the plume from the cooktop. Anything conductive bridging the contacts under the membrane reads to the control exactly like a finger that never lifts, and it is the mechanism behind most SE codes.
  3. The door switches or latchcommonThe C-d family covers door and key errors together. The giveaway for the door side is an oven that will not start with the door apparently shut, or one that cuts out when the door is nudged. Check the latch hooks on the door edge for cracks and the gap against the frame for evenness.
  4. A sensor group faultoccasionalThe C-20 family relates to temperature sensing and the C-70 family to steam sensing. The pattern that points here is an oven that runs normally on manual time and power but fails as soon as you choose a sensor, steam or auto program, because only those cycles read the sensor.
  5. Communication between the control boardsoccasionalThe C-F family. These typically arrive without any obvious trigger and often follow a power event. A reset is genuinely worth trying first, because a communication fault that clears and never returns was a start-up glitch rather than a failed board.
  6. A control lock rather than a faultcommonNo code at all, just a panel that ignores you. Look for a padlock symbol on the display. A locked keypad behaves identically to a broken one, and it gets switched on accidentally more often than anyone admits, usually by a cloth dragged across the panel during cleaning.

Fix it step by step

  1. Read the code carefully and write it down

    Note whether you have letters, numbers or both, and look closely at the first character. Samsung's display draws a capital S and a 5 identically, so SE and 5E are the same code and chasing the difference wastes time. Note also whether the oven refuses to start, stops partway, or only fails on certain programs, because the family of the code plus that behavior narrows it a long way.

    4 min

  2. Unplug it for a minute or more

    Pull the plug at the wall, or switch the breaker off on an over-the-range model where the plug sits in the cabinet above, and leave it off for at least a minute. Samsung's own guidance is that this clears some information codes. Plug it back in and watch whether the code returns by itself within seconds or only when you press something.

    5 min

  3. Clean the touch panel and let it dry

    Wipe the panel with a barely damp cloth and then dry it with a second cloth, getting right into the edges where the membrane meets the surrounding trim. Spray cleaner onto the cloth, never onto the panel, because liquid tracks down behind the membrane and produces this exact fault. Leave it a good ten minutes before testing.

    12 minTools: Two soft cloths

  4. Press every key one at a time

    Work along the panel pressing keys individually and watching for the beep and the display response. You are looking for the one that behaves differently from the rest. On an SE code that key is the fault, and identifying it turns an open-ended repair into a specific part. Write it down before calling anyone.

    8 min

  5. Test the door if you have a C-d code

    Close the door and sight along the gap against the frame, which should be even all the way down. Check the plastic latch hooks on the door edge for cracks or a missing corner, and look into the latch openings on the frame with a torch for anything lodged in them. Do not push anything into those openings, and never try to get the oven running with the door open.

    7 minTools: Flashlight

    Door interlocks are what stop the oven radiating with the door open. Never bypass, tape or defeat one.

  6. Separate sensor faults from everything else

    Run a short manual cook at a low power level with a cup of water inside. An oven that runs this perfectly but fails on sensor, steam or auto programs is pointing at the sensor families rather than the keypad or the door, and that is worth knowing before anyone quotes a part. An oven that will not run even a manual cook is a different conversation.

    8 min

  7. Check the model and region before trusting any code list

    Find the model number on the sticker inside the door frame. Samsung publishes its microwave code tables regionally, and the lists differ between markets, so a code looked up against the wrong region can point at the wrong part entirely. Check yours on Samsung's support site for the country you bought it in rather than the first list a search returns.

    8 min

How to reset it

Unplug the oven at the wall for at least a minute, or switch its breaker off on an over-the-range model with the plug hidden above, then restore power. Samsung's guidance is that this clears some information codes outright. What happens next is the diagnosis: a code that comes back on its own within seconds is reporting a real fault, while one that only reappears when you press a particular key has identified the key for you.

It still shows the code

If the panel is clean and dry, the reset has not held and no key is obviously stuck, you have gone as far as is safe from outside the case, and on a microwave that boundary is not a formality. The capacitor holds a lethal charge after the oven is unplugged, so what remains belongs to someone with a discharge tool. Make the visit short by handing over what you have already established: the exact code and its family, whether the oven refuses to start or stops partway, whether it fails on every program or only on sensor and steam cycles, and whether any single key has died. Those four things usually tell a technician which board they are dealing with before they take the cover off. There is one specific test worth asking for on an SE code: disconnecting the keypad ribbon at the control board and seeing whether the code clears. If it clears, the keypad is the fault. If it persists with the keypad unplugged, the board is. Asking for that test by name is the difference between buying one part and buying two. Then it becomes an economic decision rather than a technical one. A keypad or control board on a Samsung microwave typically runs $130 to $320 fitted, which exceeds what most countertop ovens are worth and is comfortably less than replacing an over-the-range or built-in unit, where you pay for the appliance, the installation and a cabinet opening that has to match. Samsung's own position on these codes is also worth weighing: its support pages say most microwave information codes need verifying by a technician, which is an unusually direct admission from a manufacturer and a reasonable reason not to buy parts against a code alone.

Differences between models

Two things are worth knowing before you look your code up anywhere. First, the display draws a capital S and the numeral 5 as the same glyph, so the famous SE key short error is read and written as 5E just as often, and they are one code rather than two. Second, Samsung publishes these tables regionally and says so: the lists for machines sold in different markets are not identical, so a code checked against the wrong country's table can send you after the wrong part. Use the model number from the sticker inside the door frame and your own region's support pages. Samsung also states that most microwave information codes need verification by a technician, which is a fair reflection of how little can be confirmed from outside the cabinet.

When to stop and call someone

Call a technician once a reset and a proper clean have failed to shift the code, because the remaining work is inside a cabinet that should not be opened without training and a capacitor discharge tool. Samsung's own documentation points the same way for most of these codes. Before booking, decide whether the repair is worth it: countertop ovens are usually cheaper to replace, built-in and over-the-range units usually worth fixing. Unplug the oven and stop using it at once, whatever the display says, if it arcs, smokes, smells of hot plastic, runs hot on the outside, or seems to operate with the door open.

Questions people ask

What does SE mean on a Samsung microwave?

SE is a key short error: the control has decided a button is stuck or held down rather than pressed and released, so it stops accepting input. Grease, moisture and a worn membrane cause most of them. Clean and dry the panel thoroughly, unplug the oven for a minute, then press each key in turn to find the one behaving differently.

Is 5E the same as SE on a Samsung microwave?

Yes, they are one code. The display draws a capital S and the numeral 5 with the same glyph, so the key short error gets read both ways and written both ways online. There is no separate 5E fault. Whichever you think you are seeing, the diagnosis and the fix are identical.

Can I still use my Samsung microwave with an error code?

Generally no, and it is not worth working around. Most of these codes stop the oven starting or halt it mid-cook, which is the control protecting itself. A microwave you cannot reliably cancel, or one that may be mis-reading cavity temperature, is not an appliance to gamble on. Unplug it, clean the panel, reset, and reassess.

What do the C codes on a Samsung microwave mean?

They work in families of three by subject. C-d0, C-d1 and C-d2 are door or key errors. The C-20 group covers temperature sensing and the C-70 group steam sensing. C-A codes are functional errors and C-F codes are communication between the control boards. Knowing the family is usually more useful than the exact number.

Will unplugging the microwave clear the code?

Sometimes, and Samsung's own guidance says to try it. Leave it unplugged for at least a minute so the control fully loses power. A code that clears and never comes back was a start-up or power-event glitch. One that returns within seconds of plugging in is reporting something real, and repeating the reset will not change it.

Why did my Samsung microwave show SE right after cleaning?

Because the cleaner got behind the membrane. The keys sit under a thin plastic layer and any conductive liquid bridging the contacts underneath reads as a permanently pressed button. Spray the cloth instead of the panel in future. Dry it properly, leave it ten minutes, then unplug for a minute before deciding anything has failed.

Is a Samsung microwave error code worth repairing?

That depends on which oven you own, not which code it is. At $130 to $320 fitted, a repair costs more than most countertop microwaves are worth. For a built-in or over-the-range unit the same repair is good value, since replacing it means buying the appliance, paying for installation, and finding one that fits the existing opening.

Why do different websites list different Samsung codes?

Because Samsung publishes these tables by region and the lists genuinely differ between markets. A code checked against another country's table can point at the wrong part. Use the model number from the sticker inside the door frame and your own region's Samsung support pages rather than whichever list a search puts first.
