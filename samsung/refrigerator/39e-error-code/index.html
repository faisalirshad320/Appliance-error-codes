Samsung refrigerator 39E error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Samsung refrigerator

What it means

39E is Samsung's ice maker function error. The control told the ice maker to run a cycle, waited, and did not get the confirmation back that the cycle finished, so it flagged the assembly rather than any one component inside it. In practice that usually means the harvest mechanism is jammed with ice, the motor or gearbox has given up, or the sensor that reports the ice maker's position is not being read. The refrigerator keeps cooling throughout, so this costs you ice rather than food. Start by emptying the bucket and looking for a block of ice holding the ejector arm.

Safety first

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall before any panel or cover comes off. The ice maker harness carries mains-derived power behind the liner, and a wet ice compartment and live wiring are a bad combination. Never defeat a door switch to keep the fan or the lights running while you work.

Why a Samsung refrigerator shows 39E

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Ice has jammed the harvest mechanismmost casesTake the bucket out and look at the ice maker with a flashlight. Cubes fused into a solid mass in the tray, a sheet of ice over the ejector arm, or a cube wedged between the arm and the housing all stop the cycle part way. The giveaway is an arm stopped somewhere other than its rest position.
  2. Cubes have welded together from slow ice usemost casesThe bucket contents come out as one lump rather than loose cubes. A household that uses little ice lets the cubes partly melt and refreeze into a block, which backs up into the ice maker and stalls the harvest. Common on second fridges and in winter.
  3. The ice maker motor or gearbox has failedcommonThe tray is clear of ice, nothing is obstructing the arm, and the assembly still does nothing when the cycle is called. A motor straining with a clicking or grinding noise points the same way. These are plastic gear trains and they strip, usually after years of working against partial jams.
  4. The fill is wrong, so the tray never makes a proper batchcommonCubes that come out hollow, tiny, or as a single overflowing slab. Too little water and the harvest sensor never sees a finished batch; too much and the overflow freezes the tray to the housing. Check the water supply line and the inlet valve before condemning the ice maker.
  5. The connector or harness at the ice makeroccasionalUnplug the fridge, pull the ice maker and look at the plug for green corrosion, a backed-out pin or a wire out of its crimp. The ice compartment is the dampest part of the appliance. This is the cheapest real cause and it costs nothing to check while the assembly is out anyway.
  6. The main control boardrareA new ice maker behaves exactly like the old one and the harness is sound end to end. The board commands the cycle and reads the confirmation back, so it is the last suspect. Do not let anyone start here, because it is far and away the most expensive answer.

Fix it step by step

  1. Unplug the fridge and empty the ice bucket

    Pull the plug at the wall rather than switching off at the panel, then take the bucket out and empty it into the sink. If the contents come out as one solid lump rather than loose cubes, you have already found a large part of the problem. Leave the bucket out while you work.

    8 min

    Unplug at the wall. The ice maker harness is live behind the liner.

  2. Look at where the ejector arm has stopped

    Shine a light on the ice maker. The arm should sit at its rest position. An arm frozen halfway round, or one with a cube wedged between it and the housing, tells you the cycle stalled mechanically rather than electrically. Note the position before you disturb anything, because it is the best clue you will get.

    6 minTools: Flashlight

  3. Thaw the ice maker out properly

    Melt the ice with a hairdryer on a low setting held well back, working around the tray, the arm and the housing, and catch meltwater with a towel. Do not lever at it with a knife or screwdriver; the tray and housing are plastic and they crack. Keep going until the arm moves freely by hand through its full travel.

    35 minTools: Hairdryer, towels

    Low heat and well back. Melting the liner or cracking the tray turns a cheap fix into an expensive one.

  4. Turn the mechanism by hand

    With everything thawed, move the ejector arm gently through its travel. It should turn against light resistance and feel consistent all the way round. A section that is completely free with no resistance at all suggests stripped gears; a section that will not move at all with no ice present suggests a seized motor or gearbox.

    8 min

  5. Check the connector behind the ice maker

    Take the ice maker out, usually one or two screws and a release tab, photographing it first so refitting is not guesswork. Unplug the harness and look at both halves for green corrosion, a pin backed out of its housing, or a wire pulled from its crimp. Dry it thoroughly before plugging it back together.

    22 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, phone camera, flashlight

    Fridge still unplugged.

  6. Check what the fill looks like

    Refit, restore power and watch a fill. The tray should take a clean, even charge of water that sits below the rim. Hollow or tiny cubes mean a restricted supply, so check the water line for a kink and the filter for age. Water overflowing the tray freezes the whole assembly solid and causes this code repeatedly.

    25 minTools: Flashlight

  7. Reset and give it a full day

    Unplug for five minutes to clear the stored fault, plug back in and leave it alone for twenty-four hours with the bucket in place. One successful harvest proves very little, because the failures here are intermittent by nature. A full day of normal production is the test that actually means something.

    10 min

How to reset it

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall for five minutes, then plug it back in. The control stores the fault, so 39E will keep showing after you have cleared a jam until it is reset. Then leave the fridge alone for a full day with the bucket in place, because a single successful harvest does not prove an intermittent fault has gone.

It still shows 39E

If the assembly is thawed, the arm turns freely by hand and the connector is clean and dry, the question becomes whether the ice maker is failing to run or failing to report that it ran, and those lead different places. Watch a full cycle with the bucket out and a flashlight on the assembly. An ice maker that visibly drives the arm round and ejects properly, and still throws 39E, is completing the job but not confirming it, which points at the position sensor inside the assembly or at the harness back to the board. An ice maker that does nothing at all when the cycle is called, with no motor noise, points at the motor or at power not reaching it. Next, deal with the fill before you buy anything, because a tray that never gets a proper charge of water produces a batch the assembly cannot harvest cleanly, and that looks exactly like a mechanical failure. Hollow or undersized cubes mean a restricted supply: check the line behind the fridge for a kink, and change the water filter if it is more than six months old, which is the single most overlooked cause of bad ice on these. If the mechanism is genuinely dead, the ice maker assembly is the part, and on most Samsung models the motor, gearbox and sensor are not sold separately, so it is one unit at one price. Look your full model number up before ordering, because ice makers vary across side-by-side, French-door and four-door Flex layouts and they are not interchangeable. Only when a new assembly behaves identically does the board come into it. One thing worth accepting: an ice maker that jammed because the household uses little ice will jam again, and the fix for that is using the ice or switching the ice maker off, not a new part.

Differences between models

Samsung's published list gives 39E as an ice maker function error, and the same fault appears as 39C on newer models, since Samsung changed the code suffix from E to C partway through the range. Some secondary sources describe 39E more narrowly as an optical or position sensor fault, which is a component inside the assembly rather than a different fault, so the diagnosis is the same either way. Ice maker design varies considerably between side-by-side, French-door and four-door Flex models, including whether the assembly sits in the freezer, in the fridge compartment or in a door, so check your full model number before ordering parts.

When to stop and call someone

Call someone if the mechanism is clear and free but the assembly does not move when a cycle is called, and you would rather not work out whether power is reaching it. Call sooner if you find water pooling in the fridge or running down inside a door, because an overflowing fill is a leak as well as an ice fault. There is no urgency here, since 39E does not affect cooling, so take the time to rule out a jam, a bad fill and an old filter before paying for anything. Nothing in a 39E repair touches the sealed refrigerant circuit, and any quote that mentions the compressor or refrigerant for this code deserves a second opinion.

Questions people ask

What does 39E mean on a Samsung refrigerator?

It is the ice maker function error. The control called for an ice cycle and did not get confirmation that it completed, so it has flagged the whole assembly rather than one component. The most common reason is ice jamming the harvest arm. Cooling is unaffected, so the fridge and freezer carry on working normally.

Is 39E the same as 39C?

Yes. Samsung moved its refrigerator code suffix from E to C partway through the range, so older models show 39E and newer ones 39C for the identical fault. The checks and parts are the same. Search both when looking for information about your specific model, because most write-ups only cover one of the two.

Can I still use the fridge with a 39E code?

Yes. The sealed system and the cooling are not involved, so food is safe and both compartments keep working. What you lose is ice. If the assembly has jammed with the tray full of water, check the bottom of the compartment occasionally for drips while you work out the repair.

Why does my ice keep freezing into one big lump?

Because the household is not using enough of it. Cubes sitting in the bucket partly melt and refreeze into a block, which backs up into the ice maker and stalls the harvest. It is the usual cause on second fridges and over winter. Either use the ice, empty the bucket periodically, or switch the ice maker off.

Do I need a whole new ice maker or just the motor?

On most Samsung models the motor, gearbox and position sensor come as one assembly and are not sold separately, so it is one part at one price. Check your full model number on a parts site to confirm. Clear every jam and rule out a bad fill first, because both imitate a dead mechanism.

Could a dirty water filter cause 39E?

Indirectly, yes, and it is worth ruling out early. A restricted filter gives a weak fill, which makes hollow or undersized cubes that the harvest cycle cannot eject cleanly. That stalls the cycle and the control reports a function error. A filter older than about six months is cheap to change and a common fix.

Is a 39E repair worth it on an older fridge?

Often not, if it comes down to a new ice maker assembly on a fridge already several years old. The part is one of the dearer ones in the cabinet and the repair buys you ice rather than cooling. Plenty of people clear the jam, switch the ice maker off and live with it, which is a legitimate choice here.
