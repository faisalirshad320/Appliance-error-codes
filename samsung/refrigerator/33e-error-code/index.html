Samsung refrigerator 33E error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Samsung refrigerator

What it means

33E is Samsung's ice pipe heater error. A small heater wraps the tube that carries water into the ice maker, and its job is to stop the last few drops freezing into a plug between harvests. The control has found that heater circuit outside the range it expects, so it reports 33E. What you will see in the kitchen is a fill tube blocked with a cone of ice, an ice maker that has stopped producing, and often water backing up or dripping where it should not. The fridge itself keeps cooling normally, so this is an ice maker fault rather than a food-safety one.

Safety first

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall before any panel or cover comes off. The ice pipe heater and the ice maker harness carry mains-derived power behind the liner, and a wet ice compartment and live wiring are a bad combination. Never defeat a door switch to keep the lights or the fan running while you work.

Why a Samsung refrigerator shows 33E

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. The ice pipe heater element has gone open circuitmost casesUnplug the fridge, pull the ice maker and find the heater's two-wire connector behind it. Measure across the heater on the ohms range: a reading means the element is intact, an open circuit condemns it. This is the fault the code is literally describing and it is where to start.
  2. The connector or harness at the heater has corroded or worked loosemost casesThe heater itself measures fine at its own terminals but the code persists. Look at the plug for green corrosion, a pin backed out of its housing, or a wire pulled out of its crimp. The ice compartment is the dampest place in the appliance, so this happens more than people expect.
  3. A plug of ice has built up around the fill tube and the ice makercommonLook into the ice compartment with a flashlight. A cone of clear ice growing out of the fill tube, or a solid block around the ice maker body, means the tube has been freezing shut for weeks. Clear it and you will be able to see whether the heater is holding the tube clear afterwards.
  4. The water fill valve is dribbling after the fillcommonAfter an ice harvest, water keeps seeping into the tube for a while instead of stopping cleanly. A heater that would cope with a clean shut-off cannot keep up with a steady drip, so the tube freezes anyway. The giveaway is ice reforming within a day of you clearing it.
  5. The ice maker assembly, where the heater is built into itoccasionalOn models where the fill tube heater is not a separate part, a failed heater means the whole ice maker. Check your model number on a parts site before ordering: if no standalone heater is listed, that answers it.
  6. The main control boardrareA measured-good heater, a clean connector and an intact harness, and 33E still appears after a reset. The board drives and monitors this circuit, so it is the last suspect. Confirm the heater twice before accepting this, because it is much the most expensive answer.

Fix it step by step

  1. Unplug the fridge and empty the ice bucket

    Pull the plug at the wall rather than using the control panel, and take the ice bucket out and empty it into the sink. You want the ice compartment clear and the appliance dead before anything else. Leave the bucket out so air can get to the compartment.

    8 min

    Unplug at the wall. The ice maker harness is live behind the liner.

  2. Look at the fill tube with a flashlight

    Shine a light up at the back of the ice compartment where the small plastic or metal tube enters above the ice maker. You are looking for a cone of clear ice growing out of the end of it. That cone is the symptom the heater exists to prevent, and finding it confirms the circuit has not been doing its job.

    6 minTools: Flashlight

  3. Thaw the fill tube and the ice maker

    Melt the ice with a hairdryer on a low setting held well back, working the warm air around the tube and the ice maker body, and catch the meltwater with a towel. Do not chip at it with a knife or a screwdriver: the tube and the ice maker housing are plastic and they split. Give it as long as it takes.

    35 minTools: Hairdryer, towels

    Keep the hairdryer on a low setting and well back. Melting the liner is an expensive mistake.

  4. Remove the ice maker and find the heater connector

    Most Samsung ice makers come out with one or two screws and a release tab, then unplug from the harness behind. Note how it sits before you pull it. Behind it you will find the fill tube and the heater's own two-wire connector. Take a photo before unplugging anything so refitting is not guesswork.

    25 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, phone camera, flashlight

    Fridge still unplugged.

  5. Measure the heater circuit

    Put a multimeter on ohms across the two heater terminals. Any sensible reading means the element is intact and the fault lies in the wiring or the board. An open circuit, with the meter showing no continuity at all, condemns the heater and settles the diagnosis without needing a reference figure for your model.

    10 minTools: Multimeter

    Measure resistance on a dead circuit only, with the fridge unplugged.

  6. Inspect the connector and the harness

    Look at both halves of the plug for green corrosion, a pin that has backed out of its housing, or a wire that has pulled out of its crimp. Flex the harness gently along its visible length and look for a cracked or stiffened section. Dry everything thoroughly before plugging it back together.

    12 minTools: Flashlight

  7. Refit, restore power and watch the next harvest

    Refit the ice maker, plug the fridge back in and give it several hours. Then look at the fill tube again. A tube that is still clear after two or three harvest cycles means the heater is working. Ice reforming within a day means either the heater is still not energizing or the fill valve is dribbling after each fill.

    20 minTools: Flashlight

How to reset it

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall for five minutes, then plug it back in. The control stores the fault and will keep showing 33E after you have cleared the ice or fixed the circuit until it is reset. Give it several hours and two or three harvest cycles before you decide whether the repair has held, because one clear tube proves nothing.

It still shows 33E

If the heater measures intact and the connector is clean and dry, stop thinking about the heater and start watching the water. The commonest reason a measured-good heater fails to keep the tube clear is that the fill valve is not shutting off cleanly, so water keeps seeping down the tube long after the fill has finished. A heater sized for a clean shut-off cannot out-run a steady drip. The test is cheap: clear the ice, then look at the tube a few hours after a harvest rather than days later, and if you can see fresh water sitting in the end of it with no fill happening, the valve is the fault rather than the heater. Second, check whether your model even has a standalone heater. On several Samsung ice makers the fill tube heater is built into the assembly and is not sold separately, so look your full model number up on a parts site before ordering: if no heater is listed, the ice maker is the part and the price jumps accordingly. Third, consider how much ice was there to begin with. A compartment that had frozen into one solid block has usually been failing for months, and clearing it once will not hold unless you also fix why the door seal is letting warm moist air in. Run a hand around the ice compartment door gasket and look for a gap or a section that has lost its spring. Finally, if the heater is good, the harness is good and the valve shuts off cleanly, the board is what drives this circuit and that is the expensive end. Get a technician to confirm it rather than buying one on a guess. Nothing on this page involves the sealed refrigerant circuit, so no part of a 33E diagnosis should ever require opening the system.

Differences between models

Samsung's published list gives 33E as an ice pipe heater error, and the same fault appears as 33C on newer models, since Samsung changed the suffix from E to C partway through the range. The two are identical and a search for one suffix will miss half the useful material. Where the ice maker sits varies a great deal across side-by-side, French-door and four-door Flex models, and so does whether the fill tube heater is a separate part or built into the ice maker assembly. Check your full model number on a parts site before ordering anything, and where a general chart disagrees with your own manual, believe the manual.

When to stop and call someone

Call someone if the heater measures intact, the connector is clean and the tube still freezes shut after you have cleared it, because the next suspects are the fill valve and the control board and neither is worth buying on a guess. Call sooner if you find water pooling in the bottom of the fridge or running down the inside of a door, since that is a separate leak that will reach the floor. There is no urgency from a food-safety point of view, because 33E does not stop the fridge cooling, so take the time to diagnose it properly. Nothing in this repair touches the sealed refrigerant circuit; if a technician suggests it does, get a second opinion.

Questions people ask

What does 33E mean on a Samsung refrigerator?

It is the ice pipe heater error. A small heater keeps the water fill tube from freezing shut between ice harvests, and the control has found that circuit out of range. The visible symptom is a cone of ice growing from the fill tube and an ice maker that has stopped producing. The fridge carries on cooling normally.

Is 33E the same as 33C?

Yes. Samsung changed the suffix on refrigerator codes from E to C partway through the range, so older models show 33E and newer ones 33C for the identical fault. The checks and the parts are the same. It is worth searching both when you look for model-specific information, because most write-ups only cover one.

Can I still use the fridge with a 33E code?

Yes. The sealed system, the compressor and the cooling are untouched, so your food is fine and the fridge will run normally. What you lose is ice. The one thing to watch is water: a fill tube freezing and thawing repeatedly sometimes drips inside the compartment, so check the bottom of the fridge for pooling.

Why does the ice keep coming back after I melt it?

Because melting the ice treats the symptom. If the heater is genuinely dead, the tube refreezes on the next fill. If the heater is fine, the usual reason is a water valve that dribbles after the fill rather than shutting cleanly, which overwhelms a heater sized for a clean shut-off. Watch the tube a few hours after a harvest.

Can I just turn the ice maker off and ignore it?

You can, and some people do for years. Switching the ice maker off and keeping the bucket empty stops the cycle that freezes the tube. The code may still display, which is irritating but harmless. Clear the existing ice first, otherwise the block sitting in there will melt slowly and drip somewhere you would rather it did not.

Is a 33E repair worth paying for?

It depends on which part your model needs. A standalone fill tube heater is a modest part and a reasonable repair. If your model only sells the heater as part of the whole ice maker assembly, the price roughly triples, and at that point some people decide that buying bagged ice is the better deal on an older fridge.
