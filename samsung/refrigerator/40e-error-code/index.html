Samsung refrigerator 40E error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Samsung refrigerator

What it means

40E is Samsung's ice room fan error. On models that make ice in a compartment of their own rather than in the freezer, a small fan pushes cold air into that compartment, and the control has found it is not turning at the speed it was told to. Frost packed around the fan blade is the usual reason, with a failed fan motor next. The practical symptom is ice that clumps or stops forming while the fridge and freezer stay perfectly cold, which is what tells you this is an ice room fault rather than a cooling one.

Safety first

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall before any cover comes off. The fan and the ice room harness carry mains-derived power behind the liner, and a compartment wet with melting frost and live wiring are a bad combination. Never defeat a door switch so the fan keeps running while you have your hands near the blade.

Why a Samsung refrigerator shows 40E

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Frost has built up around the fan blade and stalled itmost casesTake the ice bucket out and look behind the ice room cover with a flashlight. White frost packed around the blade, or a blade frozen to its shroud, stops it dead and the control reads the stall. This is the single most common cause and the fix costs nothing but time.
  2. A door or ice room seal is letting warm moist air inmost casesRun a hand around the ice compartment and the fridge door gasket. A section that has lost its spring, a gap you can see daylight through, or a door that does not pull itself shut over the last inch all feed humid air in, and that humid air is what becomes the frost that stalls the fan.
  3. The ice room fan motor has failedcommonThe compartment is thawed, the blade spins freely by hand, and the fan still does nothing with the door switch held in. A motor that buzzes without turning, or that turns slowly and noisily, says the same thing. These are small fans and they wear out.
  4. Something is physically fouling the bladecommonSpin the blade by hand with the fridge unplugged. A stray ice cube that escaped the bucket, a piece of packaging or a shard of plastic from a cracked shroud will catch once per revolution. You will feel it as a regular tick rather than smooth rotation.
  5. The connector or harness at the fanoccasionalThe blade is free, the motor is sound, and the fan still never runs. Look at the plug behind the fan housing for green corrosion, a backed-out pin or a wire out of its crimp. The ice room is a damp place by design, so this is worth checking before buying a motor.
  6. The main control boardrareA new fan behaves exactly like the old one and the harness is sound end to end. The board drives the fan and reads its speed feedback, so it is the last suspect and by far the most expensive. Confirm the fan twice before anyone goes near this.

Fix it step by step

  1. Unplug the fridge and take the ice bucket out

    Pull the plug at the wall rather than switching the fridge off at the panel. Take the ice bucket out, empty it into the sink and leave it out. You need clear access to the back of the ice compartment, and you do not want the fan able to start while your fingers are near the blade.

    8 min

    Unplug at the wall. The fan is live behind the liner and will start without warning if the door switch is pressed.

  2. Look behind the ice room cover

    Shine a light into the back of the ice compartment. On most models a plastic cover with a grille sits over the fan. Look for white frost packed around the blade or a solid rime over the grille. If you can see frost from here without taking anything apart, you have found the fault and the rest is defrosting.

    8 minTools: Flashlight

  3. Spin the blade by hand

    If you can reach the blade, turn it with a finger. It should spin freely and coast for a moment. A blade that will not move is either frozen in or has a seized motor behind it, and a blade that ticks once per revolution has something catching it. Note which, because it decides whether you are defrosting or ordering a part.

    5 minTools: Flashlight

    Fridge unplugged before fingers go anywhere near the blade.

  4. Defrost the ice compartment properly

    The reliable way is to empty the fridge, leave it unplugged with the doors open and towels down, and give it six to eight hours. A hairdryer on a low setting held well back speeds it up, but do not aim it at one spot, and do not chip at the frost with anything metal: the evaporator and the liner behind it puncture easily and that is terminal.

    420 minTools: Towels, hairdryer, bowl

    Never use a sharp tool on frost. Puncturing the liner or a refrigerant line turns a fan fault into a scrapped fridge.

  5. Check the door seals while you wait

    With the fridge off and defrosting, run your hand around every door gasket. Close a sheet of paper in the door and pull: it should drag. Somewhere it slides out freely is a gap, and that gap is where the moist air that became your frost got in. Clean the gaskets with warm soapy water, which restores a surprising amount of grip.

    25 minTools: Sheet of paper, cloth

  6. Check the fan connector

    With the compartment thawed and the fridge still unplugged, take the fan cover off, usually a couple of screws and some clips, and photograph it before going further. Unplug the fan and look at both halves of the connector for green corrosion, a pin backed out, or a wire out of its crimp. Dry everything before reassembling.

    25 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, phone camera, flashlight

    Fridge still unplugged.

  7. Reset and watch it for two days

    Refit everything, plug in, and reset by leaving it unplugged five minutes beforehand so the stored code clears. Then give it two days of normal use before deciding it is fixed. Frost that took months to build will come back in weeks if the seal problem is still there, and a single good day proves nothing.

    20 min

How to reset it

Unplug the refrigerator at the wall for five minutes, then plug it back in. The control stores the fault and will keep showing 40E after the frost has gone until it is reset. Then give it two days of normal use before judging the repair, because a fan freed by defrosting will stall again within weeks if the frost is still getting in.

It still shows 40E

If the compartment is fully thawed, the blade spins freely and the connector is clean, the fan motor itself is the next suspect and it is a modest part on most models. Before ordering, do the one test that separates a dead fan from a fan that is never asked to run: with the fridge plugged in and the ice room open, hold the door switch in with a finger or a piece of tape so the appliance thinks the door is shut, and listen. A fan that spins up means the motor and the board are both fine and your problem was the frost. Silence, with a blade you know turns freely, means either the motor is dead or it is not being energized, and the connector check you have already done narrows that to the motor in most cases. The harder question is why the frost was there at all, because fitting a new fan into a compartment that frosts up again just buys you a few months. Three things cause it and all are cheaper than the fan. A door gasket that has lost its grip is the commonest, and the paper test around every door will find it. A door left ajar repeatedly, usually by something on a shelf fouling it, is the second. The third is a defrost problem in the main system, and the clue there is frost appearing on the back wall of the freezer as well as in the ice room, which widens the diagnosis beyond this code. If the fan is good, the seals are good and the frost keeps returning, that is the point to get a technician in to look at the defrost circuit as a whole rather than keep replacing ice room parts. Nothing in a 40E diagnosis involves the sealed refrigerant circuit, and nobody should be opening the system to fix an ice room fan.

Differences between models

Samsung's published list gives 40E as an ice room fan error, and the same fault appears as 40C on newer models, since Samsung changed the code suffix from E to C partway through the range. This code only applies to models with a separate ice room, meaning the ice maker lives in its own insulated compartment, typically in the fridge section or a door on French-door and four-door Flex layouts, rather than in the freezer. On a side-by-side where ice is made in the freezer there is no ice room fan, so if your model shows 40E and has no separate ice compartment, check your own manual's code list before assuming this page applies.

When to stop and call someone

Call someone if the compartment is thawed, the blade is free and the fan still will not run with the door switch held in, and you would rather not work out whether power is reaching it. Call sooner if frost is also building on the back wall of the freezer, because that points at the defrost system as a whole rather than one fan and is worth diagnosing properly before you spend on ice room parts. There is no food-safety urgency, since 40E does not stop the fridge or freezer cooling. Nothing in this repair touches the sealed refrigerant circuit, and any quote that mentions refrigerant for an ice room fan deserves a second opinion.

Questions people ask

What does 40E mean on a Samsung refrigerator?

It is the ice room fan error. On models that make ice in a compartment of their own, a small fan feeds cold air into that compartment, and the control has found it is not turning at the commanded speed. Frost packed around the blade is the usual cause. The fridge and freezer keep cooling normally throughout.

Is 40E the same as 40C?

Yes. Samsung changed the refrigerator code suffix from E to C partway through the range, so older models display 40E and newer ones 40C for the identical fault. The checks and the parts are the same. Search both spellings when looking for information on your model, since most write-ups only cover one.

Can I still use the fridge with a 40E code?

Yes. The sealed system and the main cooling are not involved, so food stays safe and both compartments work. What suffers is ice production, and ice that does form tends to clump. Keep an eye on the bottom of the ice compartment for meltwater while you sort out the repair, since a stalled fan lets the compartment warm up.

How long should I leave the fridge off to defrost it?

Six to eight hours with the doors open is the reliable figure, and longer does no harm. A hairdryer on a low setting speeds it up if you keep it moving and well back. Move the food to a cooler first. Rushing it with a short defrost usually leaves ice behind the fan housing where you cannot see it.

Why does the frost keep coming back after I defrost it?

Because defrosting treats the symptom. Frost forms from humid air getting in, so the real cause is almost always a door gasket that has lost its grip or a door that does not close fully. Do the paper test on every door. If frost also appears on the back wall of the freezer, the defrost system itself needs looking at.

Can I just turn the ice maker off and leave it?

You can, and on an older fridge it is a reasonable choice. The fan serves the ice compartment rather than food storage, so leaving it is not a safety problem. Defrost the compartment first so the existing ice does not melt and drip somewhere awkward, and accept that the code may keep displaying.
