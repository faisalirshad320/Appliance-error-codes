Samsung washer 4C error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-16 · Samsung washer

What it means

4C means your Samsung washer asked for water and did not get enough within the time it allows. It is a water supply fault, not a washer fault, about four times out of five. Check that both taps are fully open and that the small mesh filter screens where the hoses meet the machine are not clogged with grit, which is the single most common cause. The code is displayed as 4E on older Samsung models and means exactly the same thing.

Why a Samsung washer shows 4C

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A supply tap is closed or only part openmost casesTurn both taps anticlockwise until they stop. If the machine was recently moved, serviced or had a leak scare, someone almost certainly turned one down and never turned it back.
  2. The inlet mesh filter screens are cloggedmost casesUndo the hose at the machine end. There is a fine mesh screen set into the inlet port. If it looks furred, brown or scaled rather than bright metal, this is your fault.
  3. A supply hose is kinked, crushed or frozencommonPull the machine out and follow both hoses. A hose crushed against the wall behind the machine flows enough to drip but not enough to fill. In winter, an unheated garage or outside wall run can freeze solid.
  4. Water pressure at the tap is genuinely too lowoccasionalDisconnect the hose into a bucket and open the tap. You want a strong, fast flow. A weak dribble means the problem is upstream of the washer, in the house plumbing or a partly shut isolation valve.
  5. The water inlet valve has failedoccasionalTaps open, screens clean, good pressure at the hose, and still 4C. The valve solenoid is open circuit or the valve is mechanically stuck. This is the point at which a part is needed.
  6. Wiring to the valve, or the main control boardrareA valve that tests fine on the bench but never energises in the machine. Check the connector at the valve first for corrosion or a pushed-back pin before condemning the board.

Fix it step by step

  1. Open both taps fully

    Reach behind the machine and turn both the hot and cold taps anticlockwise until they will not turn further. A quarter-turn lever valve must sit in line with the pipe, not across it. This clears the code more often than every other cause combined, so do it before you move the machine.

    2 min

  2. Cancel the cycle and power cycle the washer

    Press Power to cancel, then unplug the machine at the wall for five minutes. The control holds the fault state until it is cleared, so a washer that now has water will keep showing 4C until you reset it. Plug it back in and start a short rinse cycle to test.

    6 min

  3. Pull the machine out and inspect both hoses

    Walk the full length of each hose from tap to machine. Look for a tight bend, a flat spot where the machine has been pushed back against it, or a section that feels rigid and cold in winter. Straighten any kink and leave a hand's width of clearance behind the machine when you push it back.

    8 min

  4. Clean the inlet filter screens

    Close both taps. Put a towel and a shallow tray down, then unscrew the hose from the machine end by hand or with pliers; expect a cupful of water. Inside the inlet port is a fine mesh screen. Pick it out with needle-nose pliers, rinse it under a tap, scrub it with an old toothbrush, and push it back in squarely. Do both hot and cold even if only one looks dirty.

    12 minTools: Pliers, old toothbrush, towel, shallow tray

    Close the taps before undoing a hose or you will get the full mains pressure in your face.

  5. Test the flow at the tap

    With the hose still off the machine, put the loose end in a bucket and open the tap for five seconds. You should get a strong jet that fills a good part of the bucket. A weak dribble means the restriction is upstream and the washer is reporting the problem correctly, so the fix is in your plumbing rather than the appliance.

    4 minTools: Bucket

  6. Thaw a frozen hose if the machine is in an unheated space

    Close the taps and disconnect the hose. Lay it somewhere warm, or wrap it in towels soaked in hot tap water and replace them as they cool. Give it twenty minutes and try again. Never use a heat gun, a blowtorch or boiling water on a rubber or braided hose; it will split and you will find out at mains pressure.

    25 minTools: Towels

    No open flame and no boiling water on a supply hose.

  7. Check the inlet valve electrically

    Unplug the machine. Remove the top panel, usually two screws at the back, and slide it off rearward. The inlet valve sits where the hoses enter. Pull the connector off a solenoid and measure across its two terminals with a multimeter on ohms. A healthy solenoid reads a few hundred ohms; an open circuit reads infinite and means the valve is dead. Check the service sheet tucked inside the machine for the exact figure for your model.

    20 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, multimeter

    Unplug at the wall before removing any panel. Do not rely on the power button.

How to reset it

Press Power to cancel the cycle, unplug the washer at the wall socket, wait a full five minutes, then plug it back in. The five minutes matters: the control board holds residual charge and a quick off-on often does not clear the stored fault. Start a rinse-and-spin to test rather than a full cycle, so you find out in two minutes instead of fifty.

It still shows 4C

If both taps are open, both screens are clean, the hoses are straight and the flow into a bucket is strong, the supply side is ruled out and the fault is in the machine. Work in this order. First, check the connector at the inlet valve itself: unplug the washer, pull the plug off the valve and look for green corrosion or a terminal that has backed out of the housing, which is common on machines in damp laundry rooms and costs nothing to fix. Second, measure each solenoid across its terminals with a multimeter; an open circuit confirms a failed valve, and the part is a $25 to $60 item that takes about half an hour to swap with two screws and a connector. Third, if the solenoids read correctly but never energise when the cycle calls for water, the fault is in the wiring harness or the main control board, and that is where the cost jumps to a few hundred dollars. One more thing worth ruling out before you spend anything: if your model has a hot and cold inlet and the hot tap is fed from a water heater that is off, empty or isolated, the washer will throw 4C on a warm cycle while running a cold cycle perfectly. Try a cold-only cycle. If that fills normally, your washer is fine and your hot supply is not.

Differences between models

Samsung uses 4C on machines made from roughly 2017 onward and 4E on earlier ones; they are the same water supply fault and the same fix. A related 4C2 or 4E2 means the water is too hot, which usually means the hot and cold hoses are swapped, or a cold-fill-only machine has been plumbed into a hot tap. On top loaders the inlet screens are reached the same way, but the top panel is held by clips rather than screws at the back.

When to stop and call someone

Call someone if the flow into a bucket is strong but the machine still will not fill after you have cleaned both screens and confirmed the valve solenoids read correctly on a multimeter, because the next step is diagnosing the harness and control board with the machine live. Call a plumber rather than an appliance technician if the bucket test shows weak flow, since that fault is in your house plumbing and an appliance engineer will charge you a call-out to tell you the same thing. And if the machine is leaking from anywhere while this is happening, turn the taps off and stop; a supply-side leak at mains pressure does far more damage than a washing machine that will not run.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between 4C and 4E on a Samsung washer?

Nothing. They are the same water supply fault displayed differently across model generations, with 4E on older machines and 4C from around 2017 onward. Every fix on this page applies to both. A trailing 2, as in 4C2 or 4E2, is a different fault meaning the incoming water is too hot, usually because the hoses are on the wrong taps.

Can I still use the washer with a 4C code?

Not really, because the machine stops the cycle rather than running without enough water. There is no safety risk in leaving it there, but nothing will wash. If only your hot supply has failed, a cold-only cycle will often run normally, which is a useful way to confirm where the problem is while you wait for a repair.

Why did 4C appear suddenly when nothing changed?

Inlet screens clog gradually and then cross a threshold, so the failure looks sudden even though it has been building for months. The other common trigger is work elsewhere in the house: a plumber turning a stopcock down, a water meter change, or a mains repair that stirs up sediment and lands it in your screens within a week.

Does 4C mean my water inlet valve is broken?

Usually not. In most cases the valve is fine and the restriction is a closed tap, a clogged screen or a kinked hose. Only replace the valve after you have confirmed strong flow at the hose and an open circuit across the solenoid, because a new valve fitted to a blocked supply will throw the same code on its first cycle.

How do I clear the 4C code once I have fixed the problem?

Cancel the cycle with the Power button, unplug the machine for five minutes, then restart. The board stores the fault and will keep showing it until it is reset, so a washer that now has a perfectly good water supply can still display 4C if you skip this step.

Can a frozen hose cause 4C?

Yes, and it is the standard winter cause for machines in garages, outbuildings and anywhere plumbed along an outside wall. The giveaway is a hose that is rigid and very cold to the touch. Thaw it with warm towels rather than any kind of flame or boiling water, and lag the pipework before the next cold snap.

My washer fills but still shows 4C. What then?

It is filling too slowly rather than not at all, and the control times out before the water level is reached. That points at a partially blocked screen or a partly closed tap rather than a total failure, so clean both screens and check that the taps are fully open rather than cracked open a turn.
