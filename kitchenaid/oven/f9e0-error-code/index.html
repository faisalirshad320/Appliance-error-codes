KitchenAid oven F9 E0 error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · KitchenAid oven

What it means

F9 E0 on a KitchenAid oven points at the electrical supply feeding the appliance rather than at anything inside it. KitchenAid's own guidance is that the oven is wired incorrectly at the house connection, most often with L2 and neutral swapped where the oven joins the household wiring. That is why this code turns up on ovens that have just been installed, moved, or fitted after electrical work, and almost never on one that has been cooking happily for five years. A few repair sites read F9 E0 as a door lock motor fault instead, so use the install history as your separator: new or recently rewired points at the supply, long-settled points at the lock.

Safety first

A wall oven runs on a 240 volt circuit and many are hardwired with no plug to pull, so the breaker is your only disconnect. Switching the oven off at its own controls does not isolate it. Do not open the junction box or the terminal block unless you are confident identifying L1, L2, neutral and ground, and never work on the supply with the breaker on. Miswired supply conductors are the one fault on this page that can injure someone.

Why a KitchenAid oven shows F9E0

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. The oven is miswired at the house connection, commonly L2 and neutral swappedmost casesThe oven is newly installed or was disconnected and reconnected recently, and F9 E0 appeared on the very first use rather than developing over time. This is KitchenAid's own stated cause and it fits the great majority of these.
  2. A three-wire and four-wire mismatch at the terminal blockmost casesAn older house circuit with three conductors feeding an oven that expects four, or the bonding strap left in or taken out when it should not have been. Typical of a new oven dropped into an old opening without the circuit being brought up to date.
  3. A loose or backed-out conductor at the junction box or the breakercommonThe oven worked, then started throwing F9 E0 intermittently, often with lights or the clock flickering. Heat cycling loosens lugs over years, and a neutral that is barely making contact behaves exactly like one that is wired wrong.
  4. One leg of the 240 volt feed has been lostcommonHalf the double-pole breaker has tripped, or a lug has burnt out. The clock and the lights often still work on the surviving leg while nothing will heat, which makes people think the oven has failed when the panel is at fault.
  5. The door lock motor or its switch has failedoccasionalAn oven that has been in place for years, with no recent electrical work, that has recently run a self-clean cycle. Start a self-clean and listen: a working lock motor drives audibly and the door latches within a minute or so. Silence points here.
  6. The control board has failedrareSupply confirmed correct by an electrician, no recent install, and the lock motor runs normally. This is the answer of exclusion and the most expensive one, so it should never be your first purchase.

Fix it step by step

  1. Do the power reset KitchenAid recommends first

    Switch the oven's breaker off, leave it off for a full minute, then switch it back on. This is the first thing KitchenAid's own guidance asks for and it clears codes left over from a brownout or a momentary supply interruption. If the oven is on a double-pole breaker, make sure both halves move together and both are fully off before you bring them back.

    5 min

    Switch at the breaker, not at the oven's own controls. Many wall ovens are hardwired and have no plug.

  2. Work out whether this is a new installation

    This single question splits the diagnosis. If the oven was fitted, moved, swapped or had any electrical work done in the weeks before the code appeared, treat it as a supply wiring fault and go down that path. If it has sat in the same opening cooking normally for years and nobody has touched the panel, skip to the door lock checks instead. Write down which it is before you spend anything.

    3 min

  3. Check the breaker for a half trip

    Go to the panel and look at the oven's double-pole breaker. A breaker that has tripped on one leg often does not move to a clean off position, it sits slightly proud in the middle. Push it fully off, then fully on. If it trips again straight away, stop and call an electrician rather than resetting it a third time, because something downstream is drawing a fault current.

    5 minTools: Torch

    A breaker that trips repeatedly is reporting a real fault. Do not keep resetting it.

  4. Have the terminal block and junction box checked

    With the breaker off and locked out, the connection where the oven meets the house wiring needs checking for L2 and neutral being swapped, for a loose lug, and for the right three-wire or four-wire arrangement with the bonding strap set correctly for your circuit. If you cannot confidently identify each conductor at that block, this is the point to stop and call a licensed electrician. It is an hour of their time and it is the actual fix in most F9 E0 cases.

    45 minTools: Licensed electrician, voltage tester

    Breaker off and verified dead before the cover comes off. Do not test or probe the supply conductors live.

  5. Listen to the door lock motor if the oven is not newly installed

    Start a self-clean cycle and stand by the door. A working lock motor runs for several seconds and you will hear the latch travel across and stop. Silence, a buzz that goes nowhere, or a latch that travels partway and stalls all point at the lock motor or its switch rather than the supply. Cancel the clean cycle as soon as you have your answer rather than letting it run.

    10 min

  6. Check the sources against each other before you buy a part

    KitchenAid's documentation calls F9 E0 a supply wiring fault and several repair sites call it a door lock motor fault. Rather than pick one, use what you found above: new install or recent electrical work means supply, a settled oven with a silent lock motor means the lock. Buying a lock assembly for a miswired oven, or paying an electrician for a dead lock motor, is the expensive version of guessing.

    5 min

How to reset it

Switch the oven off at its circuit breaker, leave it off for a full minute, then switch it back on. This is the reset KitchenAid's own guidance asks for. Do not use the oven's own power or cancel buttons, because they do not isolate the control, and on a hardwired wall oven there is no plug to pull. If the oven sits on a double-pole breaker, confirm both halves are fully off before restoring power, since a breaker that has tripped on one leg can look closed while feeding only half the circuit.

It still shows F9E0

If the breaker reset did not hold, the order below gets you to an answer without buying anything on a guess. Start with the install history, because it is free and it decides everything else. An oven that threw F9 E0 on its first or second use after being fitted is a supply wiring problem until an electrician proves otherwise, and the specific thing to have them check is whether L2 and neutral have been swapped at the terminal block, since that is the fault KitchenAid names by name. Have them also confirm the circuit is the three-wire or four-wire arrangement the oven expects and that the bonding strap matches, because a new oven dropped into an old opening is the classic setup for this. Expect an hour of an electrician's time, which is usually $120 to $300, and note that this is a wiring fault rather than an appliance fault, so an appliance technician will often charge a call-out and then tell you to ring an electrician anyway. If instead the oven has been in place for years with nobody near the panel, go the other way. Run a self-clean and listen for the lock motor driving the latch across. Silence or a stalled latch means the door lock assembly, which is $60 to $180 as a part and sits above the oven cavity behind the top trim or the control panel on most KitchenAid wall ovens. This is also the point to ask when the oven last ran a self-clean, because the heat that cycle generates is the single biggest killer of oven lock motors and control boards, and a code that first appeared within a week of a self-clean has usually been caused by it. Only when the supply has been verified correct and the lock motor runs normally does the control board become the answer, and at $150 to $400 for the part plus labor on a wall oven it is worth having someone confirm it before you commit.

Differences between models

KitchenAid is built on the Whirlpool platform, so F9 E0 means the same thing and is diagnosed the same way on Whirlpool, Maytag and Amana ovens of the same generation, and our Whirlpool F9 E0 page covers the same ground from that side. Worth knowing before you cross-check: the documentation here is genuinely split. KitchenAid's own help pages for wall ovens and commercial-style ranges describe F9 E0 as incorrect wiring at the home supply, while several repair sites describe it as a door lock motor fault, and a couple of those sites contradict their own F9 pages. You will find both readings online and neither is obviously wrong, which is why this page routes on install history rather than asserting one. On older KitchenAid ovens using single codes rather than the two-part system, a plain F9 is a door latch fault, which is a different code from F9 E0 despite how similar they look.

When to stop and call someone

Call a licensed electrician, not an appliance technician, if the oven is newly installed or was recently reconnected, because the fault is in the house wiring and that is their trade rather than an appliance engineer's. Call them immediately and stop using the oven if the breaker trips again after one reset, if you can smell hot plastic near the junction box, or if the lugs look discolored. Call an appliance technician instead when the oven has been in place for years, the supply has been confirmed good, and the door lock motor is silent, since the lock assembly on a wall oven sits behind the control panel or top trim and is not a comfortable first repair. Either way, do not open the terminal block yourself unless you can identify L1, L2, neutral and ground with the breaker off and verified dead.

Questions people ask

What does F9 E0 mean on a KitchenAid oven?

KitchenAid's own guidance says the oven is wired incorrectly at the house electrical connection, most often with L2 and neutral swapped. It is a supply fault rather than something broken inside the oven, which is why it shows up on newly installed or recently reconnected appliances far more than on ovens that have been in use for years.

Why did F9 E0 appear right after my oven was installed?

Because that is the single most common trigger. The code is the oven reporting that the power reaching it is not arranged the way it expects, usually L2 and neutral swapped at the terminal block, or a three-wire circuit feeding an oven set up for four. Have the installer or an electrician recheck the connection before anything else.

Is F9 E0 a door lock problem or a wiring problem?

Both readings exist and the sources genuinely disagree. Use the install history to decide: an oven fitted, moved or rewired in the last few weeks is a wiring fault, while an oven settled in place for years with a silent door lock motor is a lock fault. Start a self-clean and listen for the latch to settle it.

Can I still use the oven with F9 E0 showing?

No. If the cause is a miswired supply, you have a 240 volt circuit connected in a way the appliance has flagged as wrong, and that is not something to cook around. Switch the oven off at the breaker and leave it off until an electrician has checked the connection. The risk here is the wiring, not the oven.

Will resetting the breaker fix F9 E0?

Sometimes, and it is worth trying once because KitchenAid's guidance asks for it. Switch the breaker off for a full minute and back on. If the code clears and stays gone, it was a supply interruption. If it comes straight back, the underlying wiring or lock fault is still there and the reset has only confirmed that.

Does self-clean cause F9 E0?

It can, on the door lock branch of this code. A self-clean cycle runs the cavity far hotter than any cooking does, for hours, and that heat is the biggest single killer of oven lock motors and control boards. A code that first appeared within a week of running self-clean has usually been caused by it, which is worth saying to whoever you call.

How much does F9 E0 cost to fix?

Nothing if a breaker reset holds. An electrician correcting the supply connection is usually $120 to $300 for an hour. A door lock assembly is $60 to $180 as a part, more with labor on a wall oven. A control board is $150 to $400 plus fitting, and it should only ever be the last answer.
