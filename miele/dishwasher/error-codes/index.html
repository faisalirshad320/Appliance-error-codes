Miele dishwasher error codes: what they mean and where to start

Last reviewed 2026-10-19 · Miele dishwasher

What it means

Miele dishwashers report faults as the letter F followed by one or two digits, shown on the display or, on models without one, as a pattern of flashing indicator lights with the drain pump running. Unlike Bosch, the numbers are not grouped into tidy subsystem blocks, so the number has to be looked up rather than decoded. The ones owners actually meet are a short list: F11 for a drain fault, F12 to F14 for water intake and level, F24 for heating, F70 for the base tray float, and F78 for the circulation pump. The most important distinction on this page is between a blocked drain, which leaves water inside the tub, and the Waterproof system tripping, which means water underneath it.

Safety first

Several Miele codes put water in the base of the machine, inches from mains wiring, the heating circuit and two pump motors. Switch the dishwasher off at its breaker, not just the on/off button, before removing the kickplate, reaching into the sump or pulling the machine out. Never disconnect or wedge the base tray float to get a locked-out machine running: it is the flood protection, and defeating it is how a slow leak becomes a ruined floor.

Why a Miele dishwasher shows this code

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Drain faults, where water will not leave the tubmost casesOpen the door at the end of a cycle to dirty water standing over the filter. F11 is the code, and the blockage is almost always in the sump: sludge under the filter, or something hard jammed in the drain pump impeller beneath the non-return valve.
  2. Water intake and level faults, the F12 to F14 groupmost casesThe program stops early with an empty or barely wet tub, having pumped out before it quit. Close the supply valve, undo the inlet hose at the tap end into a bucket and open the valve: strong flow puts the fault in the machine, a dribble puts it in your plumbing.
  3. The Waterproof system tripping on water in the base traycommonF70, usually with the drain pump running continuously even after you cancel the program. Take the kickplate off and look into the base with a flashlight. Standing water or a tide line means a genuine leak above the tray, which is the real fault the code is pointing at.
  4. Circulation pump faults, where nothing reaches the spray armsoccasionalF78, with silence or a brief buzz where the wash phase should be a steady whirr. Either something hard is jamming the impeller or the frequency converter that drives the pump has failed, and on newer machines that converter is built into the pump.
  5. Heating faults, where the machine runs coldoccasionalF24, or dishes and tub walls that are cold at the end of a cycle with the detergent tablet half dissolved in the dispenser. The heating relay sits on the power electronics, which makes this the one group with no safe home fix at all.
  6. Electronics, wiring and connector faultsrareA code that appears the instant the machine is powered on rather than part way through a program, or a fault that comes and goes with no pattern. Damp laundry and kitchen air corrodes connectors over ten or fifteen years, which is worth checking before anyone condemns a board.

Fix it step by step

  1. Write the code down and note where in the cycle it appeared

    Read the display properly and write the digits down, then note what the machine was doing when it stopped: before filling, during the wash, at the drain, or immediately on power-up. On models without a display, note which indicator lights flash and whether the drain pump is running on its own. That second piece of information narrows the fault more than the number does, and a technician charging by the hour will not have to rediscover it.

    5 min

  2. Decide first whether the water is inside or underneath the tub

    Open the door. Dirty water sitting over the filter is a drain problem and you work the sump. A dry or near-dry tub with the drain pump running by itself is the Waterproof system, which means water is in the sealed base tray underneath and there is a leak to find. These two situations feel similar and lead to completely different repairs, so settle this before anything else.

    5 minTools: Flashlight

  3. Power cycle at the breaker, not the on/off button

    Switch the machine off at the on/off button, then turn its breaker off for five minutes and back on. Miele's control holds a stored fault, so a machine whose problem you have already fixed can keep displaying the code until the power has been properly cycled. Five minutes matters. Then restart on the shortest or rinse program rather than a full wash, so you learn the outcome in ten minutes rather than two hours.

    10 min

  4. Clean the filter assembly, because it sits behind half of these codes

    Turn the handle of the cylindrical coarse filter anticlockwise to unlock it and lift the whole assembly out of the tub floor. Separate the coarse filter from the fine filter plate, rinse both under hot running water and scrub the mesh with a washing-up brush until light passes evenly through it. Grease films look clean and still halve the flow. Refit and turn the handle clockwise until it locks down.

    15 minTools: Washing-up brush

    Switch the machine off at the breaker before reaching into the sump. Both pumps can be energised by the control without warning.

  5. Run the bucket test on the water supply

    Close the supply valve, unscrew the inlet hose at the tap end into a bucket and open the valve for five seconds. A strong jet means the supply is fine and any intake code is the machine's problem. A weak dribble means the fault is in your house plumbing, and an appliance technician will charge a full call-out to tell you exactly that. While the hose is off, lift the mesh filter out of the connector and scrub it.

    12 minTools: Bucket, towel, needle-nose pliers, old toothbrush

    Close the supply valve before undoing the hose, or you get full mains pressure in the face.

  6. Check the sump, the non-return valve and the spray arms

    With the power off and the filter out, glove up and feel around the sump for glass chips, fruit stones and bits of broken plate. Lift the non-return valve right out rather than just raising the flap, since debris hides underneath it. Then take the spray arms off, hold them to the light and clear blocked jets with a toothpick. These three checks between them resolve most drain and circulation codes for nothing.

    25 minTools: Rubber glove, flashlight, needle-nose pliers, toothpick

    Assume there is broken glass in the sump. Glove on, and use pliers rather than fingers.

  7. Get the model and serial number before anyone orders a part

    Open the door and photograph the rating plate on the door edge or the tub rim. Miele publishes its full code tables in service documentation rather than owner manuals, which is why third-party lists disagree with each other, most visibly on F14. That model number is what turns a contested code into a definite one when you call Miele support or a technician, and it stops you buying a part for a fault you do not have.

    5 min

How to reset it

Switch the machine off at the on/off button, then cut its breaker for five minutes and restore it. The control stores the fault, so a quick off-on frequently leaves the code showing even after the underlying problem is gone. Restart on the shortest program available as a test. None of this helps while the fault is still present: a blocked filter, a closed supply valve or water in the base tray will re-post the same code within seconds. An F70 in particular will not reset at all until the base tray is dry and the float has dropped, which is deliberate.

It still shows the code

If the obvious checks have not cleared it, place the code in a group rather than chasing the number, and work the groups in cost order. Drain group first, because it is the most common and the cheapest to settle: filter out, non-return valve fully out, impeller turned by hand. Jammed is a free fix, free-spinning with a hum is a pump at $120 to $260, silence is wiring or electronics. Intake group next, because the bucket test takes thirty seconds and tells you whether to call an appliance technician or a plumber. Waterproof group after that, and treat it differently from the rest: F70 is not really a fault code, it is a report that water is somewhere it should not be, and the job is finding the leak rather than clearing the code. Dry the base tray, lay white paper towel across it, run a short program and see which part of the towel wets first. Heating and circulation pump groups last, because those are the ones you cannot test from outside and the ones where the bill lands hardest. Now the part Miele owners most need to hear, because almost nobody says it at the point of sale. Miele parts and Miele labor both cost materially more than Bosch, Whirlpool, GE or LG. A drain pump is roughly double the mass-market equivalent, a circulation pump with its integrated converter runs $200 to $420, an electronic unit is $300 to $600, and an authorized Miele call-out is commonly $150 to $250 before anyone touches anything. Fewer independents stock the parts, so you may also wait a week. People are genuinely shocked by this, and the shock is reasonable: twenty-year build quality reads like cheap ownership and it is not the same thing. The honest other side is that the economics still usually favor repair. A Miele at ten or twelve years is often only halfway through its life, so $500 spent on it buys more remaining years than $500 off a new mid-range dishwasher. Under about twelve years and otherwise sound, repair. Over fifteen, or with two major parts failed at once, get the quote in writing and think properly before you agree to it.

Differences between models

Miele's dishwasher code numbering has been broadly stable from the older G 600 and G 800 series through the G 4000, G 5000, G 6000 and G 7000 machines, but three things vary. First, machines without a numeric display report the same faults through flashing indicator lights and a continuously running drain pump, so some owners never see a number at all. Second, what Miele supplies to fix a given code changes with generation: newer circulation pumps have the frequency converter built in, so a drive failure means the whole pump. Third, F14 is genuinely inconsistent across published sources, described variously as a heater pressure switch fault, a water intake fault and a door lock fault, so that one needs your own model's service documentation rather than any general list.

When to stop and call someone

Call a technician when the fault survives the free checks and the next step would mean working on live circuits or pulling a built-in machine out of its housing: a silent pump with a clear sump, any heating fault, or a leak you cannot locate with the kickplate off. Call a plumber instead if the bucket test gives a weak dribble, or if the kitchen sink is slow and gurgles when the dishwasher pumps out, because both are house plumbing and a Miele engineer will charge a full call-out to say so. Stop using the machine entirely and call if you see water on the kitchen floor or staining in front of the plinth, since the Waterproof containment has then been overwhelmed and cabinet damage will outrun the repair bill. When you book, ask three questions: the call-out fee, whether it is credited against the work, and whether the technician will check connectors and harnesses before condemning any electronics.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between a Miele drain fault and a Waterproof error?

A drain fault leaves dirty water inside the tub, above the filter, where you can see it, and the blockage is almost always in the sump. A Waterproof trip means water is underneath the tub in the sealed base tray, which should never happen, and it points at a leak. The first is usually a free fix. The second is a search.

Where does a Miele dishwasher show its error code?

On the display panel, which on fully integrated models faces up along the top edge of the door and is only readable with the door open. Machines without a numeric display signal faults with flashing indicator lights and, for a Waterproof trip, a drain pump that runs continuously and will not stop at the on/off button.

Which Miele dishwasher code is the most common?

F11, the drain fault, by a clear margin. It means the tub did not empty in the time allowed, and the cause is nearly always in the sump: a grease-filmed filter, sludge under it, or a hard object jammed in the drain pump impeller beneath the non-return valve. Most owners clear it themselves in under an hour.

How do I reset a Miele dishwasher?

Switch it off at the on/off button, then cut power at its breaker for five minutes and restore it. A quick off-on usually leaves the stored code showing. Resetting does nothing while the fault is still present, and an F70 will not clear at all until the base tray is dry and the float has dropped, which is deliberate design rather than a bug.

Why are Miele dishwasher repairs so expensive?

Parts and labor both run materially higher than mass-market brands. A Miele drain pump is roughly double a Bosch equivalent, an electronic unit is $300 to $600, and authorized call-outs are commonly $150 to $250 before work starts. There is no real aftermarket to undercut Miele prices, and fewer independents stock the parts, so you may also wait.

Is it worth repairing an old Miele dishwasher?

More often than with any other brand. These machines are built for roughly twenty years, so a ten or twelve year old one is frequently halfway through its life and a $500 repair buys more remaining years than the same money spent on a new mid-range unit. Past fifteen years, or with two major parts gone, run the numbers properly.

Can I use a Miele dishwasher while it is showing a fault code?

No. The control stops the program and will not start another while the fault is active, which is the correct behavior in every case on this page. With a Waterproof trip you should go further and leave it switched off at the breaker until the leak is found, because the only thing containing that water is the system you would be overriding.

How often should I clean the filter?

Monthly, and it is the single habit that decides whether a Miele reaches twenty years without a drain or pump fault. Lift the assembly out, scrub the mesh with a brush under hot water, check the sump for debris while it is open, and put it back locked down. Five minutes a month prevents most of the codes on this page.
