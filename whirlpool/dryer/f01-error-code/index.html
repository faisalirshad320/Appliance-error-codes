Whirlpool dryer F01 error code: main control board failure

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Whirlpool dryer

What it means

F01 means the main electronic control board ran its own self-check, failed it, and shut the dryer down. It is the board reporting a fault in itself rather than in a motor, heater or sensor. Cut power at the breaker for five minutes first, because a single F01 after a storm or a brownout is sometimes just a scrambled control that comes back clean. If it returns straight away, you are looking at a board, and on a dryer more than about eight years old that repair is often worth more than the machine.

Safety first

The control board sits behind the console on a machine fed by a 240 volt circuit, and large capacitors on the board can hold a charge after power is removed. Unplug the dryer or switch off its breaker before taking any panel off, and do not probe a live board. On a gas dryer, close the gas shutoff valve before moving the machine.

Why a Whirlpool dryer shows F01

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A genuine failure on the main control boardmost casesF01 returns within seconds or minutes of a full five minute power-down, on every attempt. Take the console apart and look at the board under good light: a cracked solder joint around a relay, a bulged or leaking capacitor, or a brown scorch ring is the visible version of this.
  2. A one-off glitch after a power eventcommonF01 appeared the day after a storm, an outage or a brownout, and a five minute breaker reset clears it for good. If the dryer then runs normally for a week, you had a scrambled control, not a dead one.
  3. A loose, corroded or backed-out connector on the boardcommonUnplug the dryer, open the console and work along each plug on the board. A connector that lifts off with no resistance, shows green corrosion, or has a pin pushed back into its housing can make the board fail its own checks. Reseat everything firmly and retest before buying anything.
  4. Moisture or insects inside the consoleoccasionalA dryer in a damp basement, garage or unheated porch. Open the console and look for water staining, white powdery corrosion on the board, or an ant or roach nest, all of which bridge tracks and trigger self-check failures.
  5. A failing component elsewhere dragging the board downrareA new board throws F01 again, or the board fails a second time within a year. A shorted heater relay load, a failing motor, or a wiring harness chafed against the cabinet can take out a board repeatedly. At this point a technician needs to find the root cause before another board goes in.

Fix it step by step

  1. Power the dryer down completely for five minutes

    Switch off the dryer's breaker, or unplug it at the wall if you can reach the socket. Five minutes, not thirty seconds: the control holds residual charge and a quick off-on often does not clear a stored fault. Restore power, wait ten seconds before touching anything, then start a timed dry cycle. This single step resolves a meaningful share of F01 codes and costs nothing.

    8 min

  2. Note how fast the code comes back

    Run a timed dry and watch the clock. F01 within seconds of pressing start, every time, means a hard board fault. F01 only part way into a heated cycle, or only on some cycles, is worth mentioning to a technician because it points at something the board is driving rather than the board itself. Write down what the dryer was doing when the code appeared.

    15 min

  3. Unplug and open the console

    Kill power at the breaker and unplug the machine. On most Whirlpool front-control dryers the console comes apart after removing two or three screws along the top rear edge, then the panel tips forward or lifts off its tabs. On Cabrio-style top-control models the console rotates back after screws at the rear. Work slowly, the ribbon cables to the display are short and easy to tear.

    15 minTools: Phillips #2 screwdriver, nut driver set

    Unplug at the wall or switch off the breaker first, and give the board a few minutes before touching it. Do not probe a live board.

  4. Reseat every connector and inspect the board

    Pull each plug off the board one at a time and push it back on until it clicks, checking for green corrosion or a pin that has backed out of its housing. Then look at both faces of the board under a bright light and a magnifier if you have one. You are looking for a cracked ring of solder around a relay pin, a capacitor with a domed or weeping top, or a brown heat mark. Any of those is a definite answer rather than a guess.

    25 minTools: Flashlight, magnifier

    Discharge static by touching the bare metal cabinet before handling the board.

  5. Clean out moisture, corrosion or nests

    If the console is damp or has white crusty deposits, blow it out with canned air and clean the affected area with isopropyl alcohol on a cotton bud, then let it dry fully for a couple of hours before restoring power. Clear out any insect nest completely. On a dryer living in a damp space this is a real fix, not a long shot, though it may buy months rather than years.

    30 minTools: Isopropyl alcohol, cotton buds, canned air

  6. Decide between a board, a rebuild and a new dryer

    Price the main control board for your exact model number before you commit. A new board typically runs $150 to $350, a technician fitting one lands between $300 and $550 all in, and a board rebuild service that resolders the known failure points is usually $70 to $150 with a few days turnaround. A comparable new Whirlpool dryer is roughly $550 to $900. Do that arithmetic against the age of your machine before ordering anything.

    20 min

How to reset it

Switch the dryer's breaker off for five full minutes, or unplug it at the wall, then restore power and wait ten seconds before pressing anything. Start a timed dry cycle rather than an automatic one so you find out in two minutes whether the code returns. A thirty second power cycle is not enough; the control holds charge and keeps the stored fault.

It still shows F01

If F01 survives a five minute breaker reset and comes straight back, the board has failed and the real question is money rather than diagnosis. Work through it in this order. First, before spending anything, open the console and reseat every connector, then look hard at the board for a cracked solder joint around a relay pin. That particular failure is common on Duet-era dryer boards and is the one case where someone handy with an iron fixes an F01 for the price of nothing. Second, consider a board rebuild service rather than a new board. You mail the board away, they resolder the known weak points and test it, and it comes back in a week for roughly $70 to $150, which is half the price of new and often more reliable than a fresh board of the same design. Third, if you want it fixed this weekend, a new main control board runs about $150 to $350 depending on model, and fitting it yourself is genuinely within reach: unplug the machine, open the console, photograph every connector before you touch it, swap the board, plug everything back exactly as photographed. Allow an hour and a half. Fourth, and this is the honest part, do the arithmetic. A technician fitting a board on a Whirlpool dryer comes to somewhere between $300 and $550 once the call-out, the part markup and the labor are in. A comparable new Whirlpool dryer is roughly $550 to $900. If your dryer is over eight years old, has already had a heater or a motor, or is a gas model needing a technician anyway, that repair is close enough to replacement that most people should not do it. If it is three years old and otherwise perfect, fix it. One more thing worth ruling out: if a new or rebuilt board throws F01 again within weeks, stop replacing boards. Something the board is driving, usually a shorted relay load or a chafed harness, is killing them, and that needs a technician with a meter rather than a third board.

Differences between models

F01 is the single-code style used on Duet-era Whirlpool dryers and the Kenmore and Maytag machines built alongside them, roughly from the mid 2000s through the early 2010s. On Cabrio-era top-load dryers the equivalent board fault shows as a plain F1. On models using the newer two-part system, the same family of fault reads F1 E1, and on those a second code, F1 E2, points at the motor control rather than the main board. All three mean the control has failed its own checks and all three are diagnosed the same way. Do not confuse the dryer F01 with F01 on Whirlpool washers, where it refers to a different board on a different machine.

When to stop and call someone

Call a technician if F01 returns on a board you have already replaced, because something in the machine is destroying boards and the third one will go the same way. Call one if the dryer is gas, since the board work is fine but anything touching the gas valve or its wiring is licensed work. And call before you spend if you are not sure whether the board or the console is at fault, because an hour of diagnosis is cheaper than a $300 part you did not need. If the dryer is over eight years old and the quote lands anywhere near $400, get a price on a new machine at the same time and compare the two honestly.

Questions people ask

What does F01 mean on a Whirlpool dryer?

The main electronic control board failed its own internal self-check and stopped the dryer. It is the board reporting a problem with itself rather than with the heater, motor or a sensor. A power-down reset sometimes clears it if the cause was a one-off power event, but a code that returns immediately means the board has genuinely failed.

Is F01 worth repairing or should I buy a new dryer?

Do the arithmetic. A board is $150 to $350, a technician fitting one is $300 to $550 all in, and a comparable new Whirlpool dryer is roughly $550 to $900. On a dryer under five years old, repair. Over eight years old, or already repaired once, replacement usually makes more sense, and that is not a sales pitch.

Can I fix F01 myself?

Replacing the board is realistic for most people: unplug the dryer, open the console, photograph every connector, swap the board, reconnect exactly as photographed. Allow around ninety minutes. What you should not do is probe a live board or guess at the part. Match the board to your exact model number, because similar-looking boards are not interchangeable.

Will unplugging the dryer clear F01?

Sometimes, and it is always the first thing to try. Switch the breaker off for a full five minutes so the control fully discharges, then restore power and run a timed dry. If F01 was caused by a storm, a brownout or a surge, it may not come back. If it reappears within seconds, the board has failed and no amount of resetting will help.

What causes a dryer control board to fail?

Heat and vibration crack solder joints around the relay pins over years of use, which is the most common failure on these boards. Power surges, lightning nearby and brownouts take out others. Damp basements and garages corrode the tracks, and insects nesting in a warm console bridge them. Age does the rest.

Can I still use the dryer with F01 showing?

No. The board has shut the machine down and it will not run a cycle. There is no fire or shock risk in leaving it sitting there unplugged, but there is also nothing to be gained by trying it repeatedly. Unplug it until you have decided whether to repair or replace.

Is a rebuilt control board as good as a new one?

Often better, in practice. Rebuild services resolder the exact joints that are known to crack on these boards and test under load, which addresses the original design weakness. A new board of the same part number has the same weakness built in. The trade-off is a few days without a dryer while it is in the mail.

Is F01 the same as F1 or F1 E1?

They are the same family of fault across different control generations. F01 is the Duet-era style, plain F1 appears on Cabrio-era top loaders, and F1 E1 is the current two-part equivalent. All three mean the main control has failed its own checks. Diagnose them the same way and expect the same costs.
