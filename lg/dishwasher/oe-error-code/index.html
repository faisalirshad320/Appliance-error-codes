LG dishwasher OE error code: why it will not drain and how to clear it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · LG dishwasher

What it means

OE means your LG dishwasher tried to pump the water out and the water level did not drop far enough in the time the control allows. It is a drain fault, and it is a blockage rather than a broken pump in most cases. Start with the filter in the bottom of the tub and the drain hose under the sink, which between them account for the large majority of OE codes. If the machine was recently installed and has never drained properly, check whether the knockout plug in the garbage disposal inlet was removed, because that single oversight causes this code more often than any part failure.

Why an LG dishwasher shows OE

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. The filter assembly in the bottom of the tub is cloggedmost casesTwist out the cylindrical filter in the tub floor and look at the fine mesh. A greasy gray film, shredded paper labels, or a mat of food debris means this is your fault. If you cannot remember ever cleaning it, that settles it.
  2. The drain hose is kinked, clogged, or the air gap is blockedmost casesLook under the sink. A hose crushed behind a pull-out bin or squashed flat against the cabinet back drains slowly enough to trip OE. If your sink has an air gap, the little chrome cylinder on the countertop, pop the cap off; gunk packed inside it is a classic cause.
  3. The garbage disposal knockout plug was never removedcommonThis only applies to a new install or a new disposal. The dishwasher has never drained properly since the day it went in. Disconnect the drain hose at the disposal inlet and look inside the spigot: if there is a solid plastic disc sealing it, that is the whole problem.
  4. Something is jammed in the drain pump impellercommonYou hear a buzz or a short hum from the pump when it should be draining, but no water moves. Broken glass, a fruit sticker, a toothpick or a cherry pit wedged in the impeller will stall it. Water in the tub will not move at all rather than draining slowly.
  5. The drain pump has failedoccasionalFilter clean, hose clear, nothing in the impeller, and the pump makes no sound at all when the cycle reaches the drain step. Silence at the drain step is the giveaway; a working pump is audible from across the kitchen.
  6. The check valve or the sump outlet is obstructedoccasionalThe pump runs and sounds normal but the water drops very slowly or comes back. Grease and scale build up where the sump meets the hose, and the flap valve that stops water running back can seize part open or part shut.

Fix it step by step

  1. Cancel the cycle and bail out the standing water

    Press Power to cancel. There will be an inch or two of water sitting in the bottom of the tub, which gets everywhere once you start taking the filter out. Scoop the bulk of it into a jug with a plastic cup, then soak up the rest with old towels. Expect around a gallon in total on a full-size machine.

    10 minTools: Plastic cup, jug, old towels

  2. Remove and clean the filter assembly

    Take the lower rack out. In the floor of the tub is a cylindrical filter; turn it anticlockwise about a quarter turn and lift it straight up. Underneath is a flat mesh screen that lifts out separately on most models. Rinse both under a hot tap, scrub the mesh with a dish brush or an old toothbrush, and pay attention to the greasy film that blocks the mesh without looking like much. Drop them back in and twist clockwise until the filter seats and locks.

    12 minTools: Dish brush or old toothbrush

  3. Check the sump for debris you can see

    With the filter out, shine a flashlight into the round sump opening it came from. Pull out anything obvious with your fingers: bits of broken glass, labels off jars, bones, a dropped twist tie. Do not push anything further in and do not poke at it with a screwdriver, because you will damage the impeller just below.

    6 minTools: Flashlight

    Broken glass is common in here. Use a flashlight and look before you reach in.

  4. Clear the air gap if your sink has one

    The air gap is the small chrome cylinder sticking up through the countertop beside the faucet. Twist the cover off and lift out the plastic cap underneath, which usually just pulls up. Clean out any sludge with a bottle brush, and flush it through with a cup of water. A packed air gap stops the dishwasher draining even when everything else is perfect, and it gives itself away by spitting water onto the countertop.

    10 minTools: Bottle brush

  5. Straighten and clear the drain hose

    Open the cabinet under the sink and follow the dishwasher's drain hose from the wall to wherever it ties in. Look for a flat spot where a bin or a stored bottle has crushed it, and make sure it rises in a high loop up near the underside of the countertop before it drops to the disposal or standpipe. Disconnect it at the sink end into a bucket and check the flow by pouring water through it.

    15 minTools: Pliers or nut driver, bucket, towels

  6. Check the disposal inlet for the knockout plug

    If the dishwasher drains into a garbage disposal, loosen the clamp and pull the hose off the disposal's side spigot. Look into the spigot with a flashlight. A new disposal ships with a solid plastic disc sealing that port, and it has to be knocked out with a screwdriver and a tap of a hammer before a dishwasher is connected. Fish the loose disc out of the disposal afterward so it does not jam the grinder.

    12 minTools: Flashlight, screwdriver, hammer, pliers

    Switch the disposal off at the breaker before reaching anywhere near the grinding chamber.

  7. Run an empty hot cycle and watch the drain step

    Put everything back, turn the power on, and run the shortest cycle with the machine empty. Stand there for the drain step and listen: a working drain pump makes a clear whirring note for twenty to thirty seconds and the water level visibly drops. A buzz with no water movement means something is jammed in the impeller. Total silence means no power to the pump or a dead pump.

    25 min

  8. Reset the stored code

    Switch the dishwasher off at the breaker for five minutes, then back on. The control stores the drain fault and will keep showing OE even after you have cleared the blockage. Start a rinse-only cycle to test rather than a full wash, so you find out in a few minutes instead of two hours.

    7 min

How to reset it

Press Power to cancel, then switch the dishwasher off at the breaker for five minutes and back on. The fault is stored, so a machine that now drains perfectly will keep showing OE until it is reset. Test with a rinse-only cycle. If OE comes back within a couple of minutes of starting, the machine is still finding water in the tub at the start of the cycle, which means it never drained at all rather than draining slowly.

It still shows OE

If the filter is clean, the hose is clear and straight, the air gap is flushed and the knockout plug is out, work through this order. First, listen hard at the drain step, because what the pump does tells you where to go next. A buzz or hum with no water movement means the impeller is physically jammed, and that is still a free fix: switch off at the breaker, remove the filter and the sump cover screws, and lift the cover to reach the impeller. People find glass chips, fruit stickers, toothpicks and the plastic shanks of produce labels in there. Turn it by hand once it is clear and it should spin freely. Second, total silence at the drain step means either no power reaching the pump or a dead motor winding. Check the connector at the pump for corrosion first, since this sits in the wettest part of the machine, then measure the winding with a multimeter; an open circuit confirms the pump. A drain pump is a $40 to $90 part and an hour's work on most LG models. Third, a pump that sounds completely normal while the water level barely drops means a restriction downstream, and the usual spot is a check valve seized part shut or the short section of hose between the pump outlet and the point where it leaves the machine. That section collects grease and narrows over years. Fourth, if everything mechanical checks out, consider the house drain rather than the dishwasher: if the sink is also slow, or the disposal backs up, your problem is the branch line and an appliance technician will charge you for telling you to call a plumber. One cheap test before you spend anything: pour a gallon of hot water into the sink and watch how fast it clears. If the sink is slow, fix that first and run the dishwasher again.

Differences between models

Older LG dishwashers display this as 0E or OE depending on the panel font, and they are the same drain fault. The filter design differs: most models since the mid 2010s use a twist-out cylinder plus a flat mesh screen, while earlier ones use a one-piece filter that lifts straight out. On models with the drain pump accessible from inside the tub, clearing the impeller is a filter-out job; on models where the pump sits under the base, it means pulling the machine out and laying it on its back.

When to stop and call someone

Call an appliance technician if the pump is silent at the drain step and the connector and winding check out, or if the water barely moves with a pump that sounds healthy, because the next step is splitting the sump with the machine on its back. Call a plumber instead if the kitchen sink drains slowly as well, or if the disposal backs up when you run it, because the restriction is in the house plumbing and the dishwasher is reporting it correctly. If there is standing water in the tub and you are leaving the machine for more than a day, bail it out and prop the door ajar; a sealed tub of dirty water turns unpleasant quickly.

Questions people ask

Can I still use my LG dishwasher with an OE code?

No, and it will not let you. The machine stops the cycle with water still in the tub rather than running a wash it cannot drain afterward. There is no safety risk in leaving it there, but the dishes will not get clean and the standing water will start to smell within a day or two, so bail it out while you work on the cause.

Why is there still water in the bottom of my LG dishwasher?

Because OE means the drain step never completed, so the water the machine was trying to pump out is still sitting there. A small amount of water in the sump is normal between cycles and keeps the seals wet. An inch or more across the tub floor is not, and means a blockage in the filter, the hose or the pump.

How do I clean the filter on an LG dishwasher?

Pull the lower rack out, twist the cylindrical filter in the tub floor anticlockwise about a quarter turn, and lift it out. A flat mesh screen underneath lifts out separately on most models. Rinse both under hot water, scrub the mesh with an old toothbrush, and refit by twisting clockwise until it locks. Do this monthly.

Why did OE appear suddenly when the dishwasher was fine yesterday?

Drain blockages build gradually and then cross a threshold, so the failure looks sudden after months of slow accumulation. The other trigger is a single object: a fruit sticker, a shard of glass or a cherry pit going into the pump in one load. If the machine has just been installed or the disposal replaced, look at the knockout plug instead.

Does OE mean my drain pump is broken?

Usually not. Most OE codes are a clogged filter, a crushed hose, a blocked air gap or a knockout plug. Listen at the drain step before you buy anything: a whirring pump with no water movement points at a restriction, a buzz points at a jammed impeller, and only silence points at the pump itself.

Can the garbage disposal cause an OE error?

Yes, in two ways. A new disposal ships with a solid plug sealing the dishwasher inlet, and if nobody knocks it out, the dishwasher can never drain. Separately, a disposal full of waste blocks the inlet from the inside. Run the disposal with cold water before each dishwasher cycle and that second problem disappears.

How much does it cost to fix an OE error on an LG dishwasher?

Nothing in most cases, because the fix is cleaning a filter or unkinking a hose. A replacement drain pump runs roughly $40 to $95 for the part and about an hour of work. A technician visit to diagnose and fit one typically lands between $130 and $300 depending on where you are and how awkward the install is.
