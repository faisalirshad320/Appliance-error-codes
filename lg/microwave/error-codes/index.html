LG microwave error codes: how to read them and what to do

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · LG microwave

What it means

LG microwave codes are an F followed by a digit, and F-3 is the one nearly everybody is searching for. It means the control board is reading a key on the touch panel as shorted or held down rather than pressed and released, so the oven stops responding or refuses to cook. Grease, steam and a worn membrane cause it far more often than a failed board. LG does not publish a public code table for its microwaves, so if you are looking at any number other than F-3, treat the number alone as a starting point and check it against your model with LG support.

Safety first

Never take the cabinet off a microwave oven. The high-voltage capacitor inside holds a charge that can kill, it stays charged after the oven is unplugged, and it does not reliably bleed away on its own. Everything described here is done from outside the case on purpose. Do not run the oven with any panel removed, do not tamper with a door latch or interlock switch, and unplug it and stop if it arcs, smokes, smells of burning or seems to run with the door open.

Why an LG microwave shows this code

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A key on the touch panel reading as heldmost casesThis is F-3. Press each key separately and watch the display. The one that does nothing, triggers something unexpected, or needs noticeably harder pressure is the one the control is stuck on. On a panel with no obviously dead key, run a finger over the membrane feeling for a soft or blistered spot.
  2. Moisture or grease under the membranemost casesThe code appeared after cleaning the front, after something steamy, or on an over-the-range unit that sits in the plume off the cooktop below. Touch panels respond to anything conductive bridging the contacts, and a film of cleaner or condensation reads exactly like a finger that never lifts.
  3. A worn or delaminating ribbon connectioncommonCleaning and a reset make no difference and the panel is sometimes partly unresponsive rather than fully dead. The flat ribbon joining the touch panel to the board loosens and its contacts dull over years of heat cycling. Confirming this needs the cabinet open, so it is a technician's test rather than yours.
  4. A control lock that is not a fault at allcommonThe oven is fine but ignores the keypad. Look for a padlock symbol on the display. A locked panel behaves like a dead one and catches people out regularly, particularly where a child or a cleaning cloth has held a key down long enough to switch it on.
  5. A one-off glitch after a power eventoccasionalThe code arrived after a power cut, a surge or a tripped breaker on an oven that had been working. Unplug it for five minutes. If the code never returns, there was nothing wrong with the oven and nothing needs replacing.
  6. The main control boardoccasionalPanel clean and dry, no key stuck, reset done, and the code reappears within seconds every time. A technician separates this from the keypad by disconnecting the keypad ribbon at the board: if the code clears with the keypad disconnected, the keypad is the fault, and if it stays, the board is.

Fix it step by step

  1. Note the exact code and what the oven does

    Write down the code as printed, including whether there is a hyphen, and record whether the oven refuses to start at all, stops partway through a cook, or just ignores the keypad. Note whether it fails on every program or only on sensor cook. These observations narrow an LG microwave fault further than anything else available from outside the case.

    3 min

  2. Unplug it for five minutes

    Pull the plug at the wall and leave it out a full five minutes so the control loses power completely and re-reads every key at start-up. On an over-the-range model the plug usually sits in the cabinet above; use the breaker instead rather than hauling the oven down. Plug it back in and watch whether the code returns on its own or only on a keypress.

    7 min

  3. Clean and thoroughly dry the touch panel

    Wipe the panel with a barely damp cloth and dry it with a second one, working right into the edges where the membrane meets the trim. Spray cleaner onto the cloth and never at the panel, because liquid runs down behind the membrane and creates this exact fault. Leave it ten minutes to dry out before you test it again.

    12 minTools: Two soft cloths

  4. Press each key in turn to find the stuck one

    Go across the panel pressing one key at a time, watching the display and listening for the beep. You want the odd one out: no response, the wrong response, or a key that only works when pressed much harder. Note which key it is. That single piece of information changes a repair quote from a guess about the board to a specific part.

    8 min

  5. Rule out the control lock

    Check the display for a padlock symbol, which means the keypad is locked rather than broken. On most LG microwaves the lock is released by holding the key with the padlock printed on it, often the Stop or Clear key, for about three seconds until the symbol goes. The key varies across the range, so find the padlock icon rather than trusting a button name from another model.

    5 min

  6. Check the door and its latches

    Close the door and look along the gap against the frame; it should be even from top to bottom. Inspect the plastic latch hooks on the door edge for cracks or a broken-off piece, and shine a torch into the latch openings on the frame to check nothing is lodged in them. Do not poke anything into those openings and do not try to make the oven run with the door open.

    6 minTools: Flashlight

    Never tamper with a door latch or interlock. They are the only thing stopping the oven radiating with the door open.

  7. Decide whether the repair is worth it

    If the code comes straight back after a reset on a clean, dry panel with no stuck key, the rest of the work needs the cabinet off and belongs to a technician. Weigh the quote against the oven. A countertop LG microwave often costs less to replace than to repair; an over-the-range or built-in unit usually justifies the repair because replacing it means buying both the appliance and the installation.

    10 min

How to reset it

Unplug the oven at the wall for five minutes, or switch off its breaker on an over-the-range model where the plug sits in the cabinet above. That fully drains the control instead of interrupting it, and the panel re-reads every key when power returns. Watch what happens: a code that reappears unprompted within seconds is reporting a genuine failed key or board, while one that only returns when you press a particular key has just told you which key to mention when you book the repair.

It still shows the code

If the panel is clean and dry, a five minute unplug has not held and no single key is obviously dead, you have reached the limit of what can be done safely from outside, and on a microwave that limit is real rather than cautious. The high-voltage capacitor holds a lethal charge after the oven is unplugged, which is why the remaining tests belong to somebody with a discharge tool. What you can still do is make the visit short. Tell the technician the exact code, whether the oven refuses to start or stops partway, whether any single key has gone dead, and whether the fault appeared right after cleaning or a steamy cooking job. Then ask them for one specific test before they quote anything: disconnecting the keypad ribbon from the control board and seeing whether the code clears. If it clears, the keypad is the fault; if it persists with the keypad disconnected, the board is. That test takes a couple of minutes and it is the difference between buying the right part and buying both. After that it becomes arithmetic rather than diagnosis. A keypad or control board on an LG microwave typically lands between $120 and $300 fitted. Against a countertop oven that is usually more than the appliance is worth, and most people replace it. Against a built-in or an over-the-range unit it is good value, because the alternative is a new appliance plus an installation, and the cabinet opening has to match. If the code is anything other than F-3 and a reset has not cleared it, the honest position is that LG's microwave code set is not publicly documented in any complete form, so get the model number off the sticker inside the door frame and have LG support confirm what your machine is actually reporting before anyone orders a part against a number from a web page.

Differences between models

LG does not publish a consumer-facing fault code table for its microwaves, which is worth knowing before you go looking. What circulates online comes from third-party fault databases and repair trade experience, and the only code those sources agree on consistently is F-3 as a key panel fault, described as a key reading shorted or held. Several of those pages also quote a specific number of seconds the key must be held before the code triggers; that figure comes from secondary sources rather than LG, so treat it as roughly how the control behaves rather than a specification. For any other number, the reliable route is the model number from the sticker inside the door frame and LG support, not a code list.

When to stop and call someone

Call a technician once the panel is clean, the reset has not held, and no stuck key has turned up, because everything after that is inside a cabinet nobody should open without training and a capacitor discharge tool. Before booking, work out whether repairing makes sense: countertop microwaves are frequently cheaper to replace, while over-the-range and built-in units almost always justify the repair. Unplug the oven and stop using it immediately, regardless of the code, if it arcs inside, smokes, smells of hot plastic, runs noticeably hot on the outside, or appears to operate with the door open.

Questions people ask

What does F-3 mean on an LG microwave?

F-3 means the control is reading a key on the touch panel as shorted or held down rather than pressed and released, so it stops accepting input or refuses to cook. Grease, steam and a worn membrane cause most of them. Clean and dry the panel, unplug the oven for five minutes, then press each key to find the one behaving differently.

Can I still use my LG microwave with an F-3 code?

Not reliably, and it is not worth trying. A control that thinks a key is permanently held cannot be trusted to start or stop when you tell it to, and an oven you cannot reliably cancel is the wrong thing to leave running. Unplug it, clean and dry the panel properly, and reset before deciding anything else.

Why did the code appear right after I cleaned the microwave?

Because cleaner ran down behind the membrane. The keys sit under a thin plastic layer, and any conductive liquid bridging the contacts underneath reads to the control exactly like a finger held in place. Spray the cloth rather than the panel from now on. Dry it thoroughly, give it ten minutes, and reset at the plug before assuming a part has failed.

Is it safe to open an LG microwave and repair it myself?

No. The high-voltage capacitor stores a charge capable of killing you and it stays charged after the oven is unplugged, which is why microwave repair is the one job where the warning is absolute rather than cautious. Every check on this page is deliberately done from outside the case. Leave anything behind the cabinet to a technician.

My LG microwave ignores the keypad but shows no code. Why?

Check the display for a padlock symbol, because a control lock behaves exactly like a dead panel. On most LG microwaves you clear it by holding the key with the padlock printed on it for about three seconds. A child or a cleaning cloth holding a key down is the usual way it gets switched on without anyone meaning to.

Is an LG microwave error code worth repairing?

It depends which oven you have rather than which code it is. A repair running $120 to $300 is more than most countertop microwaves are worth, so replacement usually wins. An over-the-range or built-in unit is the opposite case, because replacing it means paying for the appliance and the installation and matching the cabinet opening.

What should I tell the technician when I book?

Give them the code exactly as displayed, the model number from the sticker inside the door frame, whether the oven refuses to start or quits partway, and whether any single key has stopped responding. Then ask them to disconnect the keypad ribbon at the board as a test before quoting a part. That one request often prevents a board being replaced unnecessarily.
