LG washer 1E error code: what it actually is and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · LG washer

What it means

The code on your panel is IE, not 1E. LG's display draws a capital I as a single vertical bar, which is indistinguishable from the number one, so the inlet error gets read and written both ways. It means the washer called for water and did not reach the level it wanted inside the time it allows. Start with the two things that cause most of them: a supply tap that is closed or only part open, and the fine mesh screens in the inlet ports clogged with grit.

Why an LG washer shows 1E

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A supply tap is closed or only part openmost casesTurn each tap anticlockwise until it stops, or set a lever valve in line with the pipe rather than across it. If the washer has been moved, plumbed in or had a leak scare recently, somebody turned a tap down and nobody turned it back.
  2. The inlet mesh screens are cloggedmost casesUndo a hose at the machine end. A fine mesh screen sits in the inlet port. Bright metal is fine; brown, scaled or furred means this is your fault. It happens to one side before the other, so check both even when the first looks clean.
  3. A hose is kinked, crushed or frozencommonFollow both hoses end to end. A hose flattened against the wall behind the machine passes enough to drip but not to fill. In winter a run through a garage or along an outside wall freezes, and the hose feels rigid and very cold to the touch.
  4. Only one of the two supplies has failedcommonThe washer fills on one program and throws the code on another. Cold cycles working while warm ones fail means the hot side is the problem, which is usually a closed hot tap or a water heater switched off rather than anything wrong with the machine.
  5. The water inlet valve has failedoccasionalTaps open, screens clean, strong flow into a bucket, and still the same code. The solenoid is open circuit or the valve is stuck shut. On models with steam there is a separate valve, so a machine that fills on a plain cycle but not a steam one points at that one.
  6. You are looking at a different machine's coderareCheck the badge. On LG dishwashers the same characters also mean an inlet fault, so the logic transfers. On some other brands a 1E is a water level sensor fault instead, which is a different repair entirely, so make sure the appliance in front of you is actually an LG washer before working from this page.

Fix it step by step

  1. Open both taps fully

    Reach behind the machine and turn each supply tap anticlockwise until it will not go further, or put a quarter-turn lever in line with the pipe. Do this before moving anything else, because it clears the code more often than everything else combined and takes two minutes. Cold-fill-only models have one tap, and it is still worth checking.

    2 min

  2. Reset and retest on a short program

    Press Power to cancel, unplug the washer at the wall for a full minute, then plug it back in and start Rinse and Spin. The control holds the fault, so a machine that now has water will keep showing the code until it is cleared. A short program tells you within two minutes whether water is going in.

    6 min

  3. Walk both hoses looking for kinks and ice

    Pull the washer out and follow each hose from tap to machine. Look for a tight bend, a flat spot where the cabinet has been pushed back against the hose, or a stiff cold section in winter. Straighten what you can and leave a hand's width of clearance behind the machine. A hose that has been bent in the same place for years is set and needs replacing rather than straightening.

    10 min

  4. Clean the inlet mesh screens

    Close both taps first. Put a towel and a shallow tray under the connections, unscrew each hose at the machine end and expect a cupful of water. Hook the mesh screen out of the inlet port with needle-nose pliers, rinse it, scrub it with an old toothbrush and push it back in squarely. Do both sides, then hand-tighten the hoses and watch for weeps.

    15 minTools: Pliers, old toothbrush, towel, shallow tray

    Close the taps before undoing a hose or you get full mains pressure in your face.

  5. Bucket test the flow

    With a hose off the machine, put the loose end in a bucket and open the tap for five seconds. A healthy supply fills a good part of the bucket. A dribble means the restriction is upstream in your plumbing and the washer is reporting it correctly, in which case the fix is a plumber rather than an appliance repair.

    5 minTools: Bucket

  6. Check the inlet valve electrically

    Unplug the machine at the wall. Take the top panel off, usually two or three screws along the back edge, sliding it rearward. The valve block sits behind where the hoses enter. Pull a connector off a solenoid and measure across its terminals with a multimeter on ohms. Open circuit means that solenoid is dead. Use the figure on the service sheet folded inside the machine rather than a number from a forum.

    25 minTools: Phillips screwdriver, multimeter

    Unplug at the wall before removing any panel. The power button does not isolate the control board.

How to reset it

Press Power to cancel, unplug the washer at the wall for a full minute, then plug it back in. The board stores the fault, so a machine with a perfectly good supply keeps showing it until it is cleared. Test with Rinse and Spin rather than a full wash so you know within two minutes whether water is entering.

It still shows 1E

If the taps are fully open, the screens are clean, the hoses are straight and the bucket test gives a hard jet, the supply is ruled out and the fault is inside the machine. Try a cold-only program first: a washer that fills perfectly on cold and fails only on warm has a hot supply problem, not a washer problem, and the usual answer is a hot tap nobody opened or a water heater left off after a vacation. Next, unplug the machine, take the top panel off and look at the connectors on the inlet valve for green corrosion or a pin that has backed out of its housing; damp laundry rooms do this and it costs nothing. Then measure each solenoid. An open circuit confirms a failed valve, which is a $25 to $60 part and roughly half an hour of work. If the solenoids read correctly and you can hear water going in while the code still appears, the machine is filling but cannot tell: pull the thin clear hose running from the bottom of the outer tub to the water level sensor and blow through it, because detergent sludge in that hose produces a fault that looks exactly like a supply failure. Only after the valve, the harness and the level sensor all check out is the main control board a reasonable suspect, and that is a few hundred dollars fitted.

Differences between models

There is no separate 1E fault on LG washers. The character is a capital I on a seven-segment display, which is drawn as a single vertical bar and reads as a one. The same applies to LG dishwashers, where IE is also an inlet fault and gets written 1E for the same reason. Be careful transferring the code between brands: on some other manufacturers' machines a 1E refers to the water level sensor rather than the fill, which is a different part and a different repair.

When to stop and call someone

Call an appliance technician when the bucket test gives a strong jet, both screens are clean, and the machine still will not fill, because the next step is checking whether the valve is actually being energised with the machine live. Call a plumber instead if the bucket test gives a weak dribble, since the restriction is in your house plumbing and an appliance engineer will charge a call-out to tell you exactly that. Turn the taps off and stop if you find a hose weeping or split while you are back there; a supply-side leak at mains pressure does far more damage than a washer that will not start.

Questions people ask

Is 1E a real error code on an LG washer?

Not as a separate fault. What you are reading is IE, the inlet error, and LG's display draws a capital I as a single vertical bar that looks exactly like a one. The machine is telling you it could not fill with water in the time it allows. Every fix for IE applies, whichever way the characters look to you.

Can I still use my washer with a 1E or IE code?

Not for a full wash, because the machine stops rather than running without enough water. There is no safety risk. If only the hot supply has failed, a cold-only cycle will often run normally, which both gets your laundry done and proves where the problem is while you wait for parts or a repair.

What is the first thing to check for a 1E code?

The taps. Reach behind the machine and turn both fully anticlockwise, or set a lever valve in line with the pipe. A closed or partly closed tap causes more of these codes than every other reason put together, takes two minutes to rule out, and costs nothing. Then reset the washer, because it holds the fault until cleared.

Why does my LG washer show 1E when water is going in?

It is filling too slowly, or it cannot tell it is filling. A partly clogged mesh screen passes water but not fast enough, so the control times out. Less often the thin hose feeding the water level sensor is blocked with detergent sludge, so the board never sees the level rise even though the drum is filling normally.

Does 1E mean I need a new inlet valve?

Usually not. In most cases the valve is fine and the restriction is a closed tap, a clogged screen or a kinked hose. Only replace it once you have confirmed a strong flow from the hose into a bucket and an open circuit across the solenoid, because a new valve fitted to a restricted supply throws the same code on its first cycle.

Is 1E on an LG dishwasher the same thing?

Broadly yes: it is also an inlet fault, and the same logic applies about the capital I being drawn as a bar. The checks differ in detail, because a dishwasher has one supply rather than two and its inlet screen sits in a different place, so work from a dishwasher guide rather than this page for the specifics.

Why did this appear suddenly when nothing changed?

Inlet screens clog gradually and then cross a threshold, so the failure looks sudden after months of building. The other usual trigger is work elsewhere: a plumber turning a stopcock down, a meter swap, or a mains repair up the street that stirs sediment into the supply and lands it in your screens within a week.
