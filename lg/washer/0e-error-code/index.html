LG washer 0E error code: what it actually is and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · LG washer

What it means

The code is OE, with the letter O, not a zero. LG's display draws the two almost identically, so the drain error gets written both ways. It means the washer tried to pump the water out, did not get the tub empty within the time it allows, and stopped with the water still in it. On a front loader the cause is a clogged drain pump filter far more often than anything else: open the small access door at the bottom front right, drain the tub through the little tube first, then unscrew the round filter and clear it out.

Why an LG washer shows 0E

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. The drain pump filter is cloggedmost casesUnscrew the round filter at the bottom front of the machine. If it comes out wearing a coat of gray felt, hair and coins rather than looking like clean plastic, that is the fault. A filter that has never been cleaned in several years is normal and is exactly what produces this code.
  2. The drain hose is kinked or pushed too far down the standpipecommonPull the machine out and follow the hose. A hose crushed flat behind the cabinet, or shoved a foot into the standpipe so its end sits in water, both stop the pump moving anything. The hose should go in only far enough to stay put, with air around it.
  3. The standpipe, trap or sink drain is blockedcommonTake the drain hose out of the standpipe, put it in a bucket and run a drain cycle. If the washer empties happily into the bucket, the machine is fine and your house drain is blocked with the hard gray coating of lint and detergent that builds up in laundry standpipes.
  4. Something is jammed in the pump impellercommonWith the filter out and the machine unplugged, reach in and turn the impeller with a finger. It should spin freely with a faint magnetic notchiness. Locked solid, or fabric wrapped around it, means something got past the filter. A bra underwire is the classic.
  5. Too many sudsoccasionalFoam visible through the door, usually after regular detergent in an HE machine, or too much of it. A pump cannot move foam, so it spins without shifting water and the control times out. You will often have seen SUD earlier in the same cycle.
  6. The drain pump has failedoccasionalFilter clean, hose clear, impeller free, and on a drain cycle you hear either nothing or a hum with no water moving. A pump that is dead electrically is silent; one with a seized rotor hums and warms up.

Fix it step by step

  1. Unplug the machine and empty the tub

    Press Power and pull the plug at the wall. Put a shallow tray and a pile of towels on the floor in front. Open the small access door at the bottom front right with a coin or flat screwdriver, pull out the short black tube, hold it over the tray and remove its cap. A full tub is several gallons, so plug the tube between trips rather than trying to catch it all at once.

    15 minTools: Shallow tray, towels, flat screwdriver

    Unplug first. If the cycle stopped during a hot wash the water will still be hot.

  2. Remove and clean the pump filter

    Once the tub is empty, turn the large round filter anticlockwise and draw it straight out; expect another cupful of water. Pick out coins, hair, buttons and compacted gray lint, rinse it under a tap and scrub the threads. Shine a torch into the empty housing and clear anything sitting in the bottom. Screw it back clockwise until firm, and run the first wash with a towel under the door in case it weeps.

    15 minTools: Torch, old toothbrush

    A filter that is not fully home empties the whole wash onto your floor.

  3. Turn the impeller by hand

    With the filter out and the machine still unplugged, put a finger into the housing and find the small plastic propeller at the back. Turn it. It should rotate freely with a faint magnetic step. If it will not move, work out what is wrapped around it before putting the filter back; running the pump against a jam burns it out.

    5 minTools: Torch

    Machine unplugged. Anything wedged in the impeller may be a broken underwire with sharp ends.

  4. Check the drain hose along its length

    Pull the machine forward and follow the hose to where it discharges. Straighten any kink, look for a flat spot where the cabinet has been pushed back against it, and check it enters the standpipe only a few inches rather than being jammed down into the trap water. Keep the loop at the back of the machine; it is what stops the drain siphoning mid-cycle.

    10 min

  5. Test the house drain with a bucket

    Take the hose out of the standpipe, put it in a large bucket and run Drain and Spin. If the washer pumps into the bucket normally, the machine is fine and your standpipe is blocked. Pour a kettle of hot water and a cup of washing soda down it, leave it half an hour and flush. Hold the hose in the bucket or the pump will throw it out onto the floor.

    20 minTools: Large bucket

  6. Run a drain cycle and listen

    With the filter refitted and the machine plugged in, start Drain and Spin with the drum empty and put your ear near the bottom front. A working pump makes a steady whirr that changes note as it catches water. Silence means the pump is not being energised. A hum with no flow means it has power but the rotor is stuck or the pump is airlocked.

    8 min

How to reset it

Unplug the washer at the wall for a minute, plug it back in, and run Drain and Spin rather than a full cycle so you find out in three minutes rather than an hour. The code clears once the machine completes a drain successfully. If the drum is still full, empty it through the filter tube first, because a cycle started on a full tub usually just throws the same code again.

It still shows 0E

If the filter is clean, the impeller turns freely, the hose is straight and the machine drains into a bucket just as badly as into the standpipe, the plumbing is ruled out and the fault is in the washer. Work upwards in cost. First, check the pump's electrical connector: unplug the machine, take off the lower front panel or kick plate, and look at the two-wire plug on the pump for corrosion or a pin backed out of its housing. Damp laundry rooms cause this and it is free to fix. Second, measure across the pump motor terminals with a multimeter; a dead winding reads open circuit. Use the figure on the service sheet folded inside the machine rather than a number off a forum, because guessing it is how people buy parts they did not need. A drain pump is $30 to $80 and is a genuinely approachable swap: a few screws, one connector and two hose clamps. Third, if the pump reads correctly and clearly runs but the code still appears once the tub is empty, the machine is being told there is water left. Pull the thin clear hose that runs from the bottom of the outer tub to the water level sensor and blow through it; sludge in there is common and produces a fault that otherwise looks like a dead board. Only after all of that is the control board the reasonable answer, and that is a few hundred dollars and a conversation about the age of the machine.

Differences between models

There is no separate zero-E fault on LG washers; the character is the letter O and the display makes them look the same. The practical difference across the range is the filter. LG front loaders all have a user-accessible drain pump filter behind a small door at the bottom front, but most LG top loaders in the WT series do not, so on a top loader the order of work changes: check the drain hose and the standpipe first, then get at the pump from underneath or behind. Some LG models log the same fault as E03 or E21 in service mode.

When to stop and call someone

Call an appliance technician if the filter is clean, the hose is clear, and the machine either makes no sound on a drain cycle or hums without moving water, and you would rather not take the lower panel off to reach the pump. Call a plumber instead if the bucket test showed the washer draining normally, because the blockage is in your house drain and an appliance engineer will charge you a call-out to say so. Deal with it promptly either way: every failed cycle leaves several gallons of dirty water standing against the door seal, and a week of that is how a laundry room starts to smell.

Questions people ask

Is 0E the same as OE on an LG washer?

Yes. It is the letter O for the drain error, and LG's display makes an O and a zero look nearly identical, which is why both spellings are everywhere online. There is no separate zero-E code on LG washers. Everything written for OE applies exactly, whichever way you read it off the panel.

Can I still use my LG washer with a 0E code?

No, not usefully. The machine will not spin with water in the tub, so every cycle ends with soaking laundry and a full drum you have to empty by hand. There is no electrical danger, but leaving dirty water standing in the machine for days makes the door seal smell within about a week.

How do I drain an LG washer by hand?

Unplug it, open the small access door at the bottom front right, and pull out the short black tube next to the round filter. Hold it over a shallow tray, take its cap off, and let it run, plugging the tube between trips because the tray fills several times. Only unscrew the round filter once the water has stopped.

Why does my washer drain but still show 0E?

It is draining too slowly rather than not at all, and the control times out before the tub is empty. A partly clogged filter, a sagging length of drain hose or a lint-coated standpipe all cut the flow without stopping it. Less often the water level sensor hose is blocked and the machine cannot tell the tub has emptied.

Can too much detergent cause a 0E code?

Yes. A pump cannot move foam, so a drum full of suds leaves it spinning on air while the water stays put. Regular detergent in an HE machine, or too much HE detergent, both do it. Run a Tub Clean cycle with the drum empty and cut down to about a tablespoon of liquid for a normal load.

Does 0E mean the drain pump has failed?

Usually not. Most of the time the pump is healthy and something is blocking it, in the filter, in the impeller or further down the drain. Only replace the pump after the filter is clean, the impeller turns freely by hand, and the machine drains into a bucket no better than into the standpipe.

Why did 0E appear suddenly when the washer was fine yesterday?

Pump filters clog gradually and then cross a line, so a machine goes from fine to failed in a single load. The other common trigger is one object: a coin, a hair tie or a bra underwire reaching the impeller. Check the filter before assuming any part has broken, because it is free and it is usually the answer.
