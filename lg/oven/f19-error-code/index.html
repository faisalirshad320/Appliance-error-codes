LG oven F19 error code: a heating fault, usually in the lower cavity

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · LG oven

What it means

F19 means the control started a heating cycle, watched the cavity sensor, and never saw the temperature climb the way it should. It is a no-heat fault rather than a sensor or door fault. On double ovens it is reported most often on the lower cavity, which is where the main bake heat comes from, and on gas models that normally points at an igniter that still glows but no longer opens the gas safety valve. LG's own guidance is that F19 needs a service call, and on a gas oven that is the right answer.

Safety first

Anything involving the gas burner, the gas safety valve or the igniter on a gas oven goes to a licensed technician. Do not remove the oven floor panel to inspect a gas burner, do not try to light it by hand, and if you smell gas, shut off the appliance gas valve, ventilate, and call a professional. On an electric oven, switch the range off at its double-pole breaker before touching anything inside the cavity; the circuit is 240 volts and the terminals stay dangerous after the oven is turned off at the panel.

Why an LG oven shows F19

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. Gas oven: the lower igniter is weak and the safety valve never opensmost casesStart a bake and watch through the closed door with the oven light on. The igniter glows bright orange and keeps glowing well past ninety seconds with no flame and no heat. An igniter can look perfectly healthy and still be too weak to pull the valve open, which is exactly what this code catches.
  2. Electric oven: the bake element has failedmost casesWith the oven cold and the breaker off, look over the element on the cavity floor with a flashlight. A blister, split, bright burn or sagging section is conclusive. Check both ends where the element passes through the rear wall, which is where breaks hide.
  3. A relay on the control board never switches the heat oncommonThe element or igniter is proven good and nothing happens at all when the bake starts: no click, no glow, no warmth. On a double oven, the clue is that the other cavity heats normally while this one does nothing, which rules out the supply and points inside the control.
  4. Electric oven: one leg of the 240-volt supply is missingcommonThe display, the clock and the oven light all work, because those run on 120 volts, but neither bake nor broil produces any heat in either cavity. Switch the double-pole breaker fully off and firmly back on before you go any further.
  5. The cavity temperature sensor or its wiring is faultyoccasionalThe oven is noticeably warm when you open it but the control insists it is not heating, or the displayed temperature is obviously wrong. The sensor is the thin metal probe projecting from the upper rear wall of the cavity.
  6. A thermal cut-out has opened after an overheatrareThe oven ran very hot, most often during a self-clean, and then went completely dead to heat while everything on the panel still works. This is a protective device that has done its job, so the reason it overheated needs finding too.

Fix it step by step

  1. Note which cavity and which fuel

    On a double oven, work out which cavity was heating when F19 came up, and whether that cavity is gas or electric. A dual-fuel range has gas burners on top and an electric oven below, which trips people up. Everything below branches on these two answers, and a technician will ask you both in the first thirty seconds of the call.

    3 min

  2. Clear the code and reset at the breaker

    Press Clear/Off and try a plain bake at 350 in the affected cavity. If F19 returns, switch the range off at its breaker for five full minutes, then on again, and try once more. A one-off control glitch clears here. A fault that comes back on the second attempt is real hardware and further resets will not change it.

    10 min

  3. Test the other cavity

    On a double oven, run a plain bake in the cavity that did not throw the code. If that one heats normally, you have ruled out the gas supply, the electrical supply and most of the shared control in one five-minute test, and narrowed it to the failing cavity's own element or burner, sensor and relay. Write down the result.

    12 min

  4. Electric: check both halves of the breaker and inspect the element

    Switch the double-pole breaker fully off, then firmly back on; one half can fail while the other still feeds the clock and the light. If that does not fix it, leave the breaker off, pull the racks and shine a flashlight along the whole bake element looking for a break, blister or burn mark. A visibly broken element is the end of the diagnosis.

    12 minTools: Flashlight

    Breaker off at the panel before reaching into the cavity. Do not remove any rear panel.

  5. Gas: confirm supply and watch one ignition attempt through the closed door

    Light a cooktop burner first; if it lights normally, gas is reaching the appliance. Then start a bake with the oven light on and time the igniter from first glow. A healthy system lights the burner within about a minute. Still glowing past ninety seconds with no flame is the answer, and that is where your part of the job ends.

    8 minTools: Phone timer

    Watch through the glass only. Do not lift the oven floor panel, do not light the burner by hand, and stop at once if you smell gas.

  6. Clear the cavity and check the sensor probe

    Take out foil laid on the oven floor, oven liners, stored cookware and any permanently parked pizza stone. Then check the thin metal sensor probe in the upper rear wall stands clear and is not touching a rack or the cavity roof. A probe pressed against metal reads the metal rather than the air, and foil on the floor traps heat where it does not belong.

    8 minTools: Flashlight, damp cloth

    Breaker off at the panel first.

How to reset it

Press Clear/Off, then switch the range off at its breaker for five full minutes and back on. The control latches the fault and a quick off-and-on frequently leaves it in place. Test with a plain bake at 350 in the affected cavity and stay in the room. If F19 comes back on the second attempt, stop resetting, and on a gas oven stop running it altogether, because each attempt is another unsuccessful ignition cycle.

It still shows F19

If the reset did not hold, the branch depends on fuel and the honest answer for most people is that F19 ends in a service call. On a gas oven, stop after watching one ignition attempt. If the igniter glows and nothing lights, it has weakened past the point where it can open the gas safety valve and it needs replacing, but that work sits on the gas valve and the burner assembly and belongs to a licensed technician; the part is modest and the labor is what you are paying for. If the igniter never glows at all, the circuit feeding it, the relay board or the harness is at fault, which is also a technician's job. On an electric oven you have more room. First rule out a lost supply leg by switching the double-pole breaker firmly off and on. Second, inspect and then continuity-test the bake element with the breaker off and the terminals disconnected; an element that reads open circuit is confirmed dead, and at $40 to $120 with two screws and two push-on terminals it is a reasonable job to do yourself. Third, if the element tests fine and never gets energised, the relay board is next and the cost jumps to the $200 to $400 range fitted, which on an older range is the point to weigh repair against replacement. Fourth, if the oven clearly does get warm and the control still reports no heat, the sensor or its wiring is misreporting, and that is a cheaper repair than a board. One useful thing to do before you book anyone: run the controlled test, note how many minutes pass before the code appears, whether you heard a relay click, and whether the cavity felt warm at all. Those three observations typically save the first half of a diagnostic visit.

Differences between models

On several LG model series F19 is the lower cavity's version of the same no-heat check that F9 reports, which is why it turns up most often on gas double ovens where the lower cavity carries the main bake burner. LG's published guidance for F19 is that it needs service rather than a user fix. On a dual-fuel range the oven is electric even though the cooktop is gas, so follow the electric branch. The exact temperature rise and time window the control looks for differs between model series and is given in the service documentation for your model rather than on the panel.

When to stop and call someone

Call a licensed technician for any gas oven showing F19 once you have confirmed the cooktop lights and watched one ignition attempt. The repair sits on the burner and the gas safety valve, and a badly fitted igniter or a half-seated valve leaks gas into an enclosed cabinet. On an electric oven, call when the element tests good and still never gets switched on, because the next step is checking for voltage at the element terminals on a live 240-volt circuit. Call immediately, before anything else, if you smell gas, see scorching or melted insulation near a terminal, or the breaker trips when you try to bake.

Questions people ask

What does F19 mean on an LG oven?

It is a heating fault. The control started a bake, watched the cavity sensor and never saw the temperature rise as expected, so it shut the heat off. It reports that the oven is not heating; it does not say which part stopped it. On double ovens it usually refers to the lower cavity.

Is F19 the same as F9?

They report the same condition, which is that the oven did not reach temperature in the time allowed. On several LG model series F19 is the lower cavity's version of that check. The diagnosis follows the same branches: igniter on gas, element or relay board on electric, with the sensor as a less common cause on both.

Can I fix F19 myself?

On an electric oven, sometimes. Checking both halves of the breaker and inspecting or replacing a bake element are reasonable home jobs with the power off. On a gas oven, no. The usual cause is a weak igniter at the burner, and replacing it means working on the gas safety valve, which is licensed work for good reason.

Can I still use the cooktop and the other oven?

Yes to the cooktop on a gas range, since the surface burners are independent of the oven burner. On a double oven, the cavity that still heats normally is safe to use; the fault is in the one that threw the code. The affected cavity should be left alone until it is repaired.

Why did F19 show up straight after a self-clean?

Self-clean drives the cavity far hotter than normal baking, and it is the single most common thing that finishes off a marginal element, igniter or thermal cut-out. If the oven worked before the clean and not after, mention that to the technician, because it narrows the diagnosis considerably.

Is an LG oven with F19 worth repairing?

Usually yes if it comes down to an igniter or an element, since both are modest parts and the range is otherwise sound. It is a closer call if the relay board is at fault, where parts and labor together can approach $400. Weigh that against the age of the range and whether the other cavity is also showing its age.
