Bosch dishwasher E22 error code: what it means and how to fix it

Last reviewed 2026-10-15 · Bosch dishwasher

What it means

E22 means the filters in the bottom of the tub are blocked and water can no longer pass through them at the rate the machine expects. It is the mildest of the Bosch drain-side codes and usually the easiest to fix: lift the filter assembly out, scrub the flat mesh screen, clear the sump, and put it back locked down. A filter that was refitted loose or left out entirely produces the same code as a dirty one, so check the seating as carefully as you check the dirt.

Why a Bosch dishwasher shows E22

These are the causes in the order you are most likely to hit them. Work down the list, do not skip to the expensive one.

  1. A grease and food mat has built up on the flat mesh screenmost casesLift the flat screen out and hold it up to a window. A clean one lets light through the whole mesh. If you can see a gray or brown film blocking it, and the underside feels slick rather than gritty, that is the fault. The underside is where the mat forms, and it is the side almost nobody looks at.
  2. The filter is not locked into placecommonGive the cylindrical filter a gentle upward tug. If it lifts without twisting, it was never engaged. A filter sitting proud lets coarse debris bypass straight into the pump inlet and leaves the machine reading a restriction where it did not expect one.
  3. Coarse debris has collected in the sump under the filterscommonWith both filters out, shine a flashlight into the sump well. Fruit pits, olive stones, bottle labels, bone chips and broken glass settle in there and partly block the inlet. If you can see solids in the bottom of the well, clear them before you blame anything else.
  4. Limescale has furred the mesh in a hard water areaoccasionalThe mesh looks pale and crusty rather than greasy, and it does not come clean with hot water and a brush. If your kettle furs up quickly, this is plausible. Scale on the mesh blocks it just as effectively as grease but needs a descaler rather than a scrub.
  5. The filter mesh is torn or the cylinder is crackedrareHold the flat screen flat and look across it at a shallow angle for a split, and check the cylinder for a crack along a seam. A torn filter lets debris through to the pump, so you tend to see it alongside a drain fault rather than on its own.

Fix it step by step

  1. Cancel the cycle and bail out any standing water

    Hold Start for about three seconds to cancel the program. If there is water sitting over the filter, scoop it into a bucket with a plastic cup and mop the last of it with a sponge, because you cannot judge how blocked a filter is while it is underwater and you will not enjoy wearing it.

    8 minTools: Plastic cup, bucket, sponge

  2. Lift out the filter assembly

    Reach into the sump, grip the cylindrical filter and turn it anticlockwise about a quarter turn, then pull it straight up. The flat mesh screen underneath lifts out after it, sometimes with a gentle wiggle. Take both to the sink. Note which way round the flat screen sat, because it only works one way up.

    5 min

  3. Scrub both parts, especially the underside

    Run both under the hottest tap you have and scrub the mesh with a dish brush or an old toothbrush and a drop of washing-up liquid. Concentrate on the underside of the flat screen, where the soft gray mat sits. Keep going until you can hold the screen up to a light and see brightness through the whole of it, not just the edges. If it is crusty rather than greasy, soak it for twenty minutes in white vinegar or a limescale remover first.

    12 minTools: Dish brush or old toothbrush, white vinegar if scaled

  4. Clear the sump well

    Put a glove on, shine a flashlight into the empty sump and pick out everything solid with needle-nose pliers: pits, labels, bone, bits of plastic and glass. Broken glass is the thing to expect, so look before you reach. Wipe round the seat the flat screen drops into so nothing stops it sitting down level.

    8 minTools: Work glove, needle-nose pliers, flashlight

    Look into the sump with a light before reaching in. There is often broken glass down there.

  5. Refit the filters and lock the cylinder down

    Drop the flat screen back the way it came out so it sits flush in its seat with no corner raised, then set the cylinder into it and turn clockwise until it stops. Tug it upward: it should not lift. A filter that is only resting in place gives you the same code again within a cycle or two, which is why so many people clean the filter twice before the fault goes.

    5 min

  6. Reset and run a short cycle to test

    Cut power at the breaker for five minutes and restore it so the stored fault clears, then start the shortest program you have and stay with it for the first few minutes. Water should fill, circulate without an unusual whine, and drain away leaving only a film in the sump well.

    10 min

How to reset it

Hold the Start button for roughly three seconds to cancel the program. Once the filters are clean and refitted, switch the machine off at its breaker for five minutes and back on so the control drops the stored fault. Test with the shortest cycle rather than a full wash so you find out within a few minutes.

It still shows E22

If the filters are visibly clean and properly locked down and E22 is still there, the restriction is further along and the filter was only the part you could see. Work in this order. First, take the filters back out and check the plastic pump cover in the floor of the sump: it has to sit flush and lock, and one that was left loose after an earlier clean produces a code that looks exactly like a blocked filter. Second, with the cover off, look into the pump housing with a flashlight and turn the impeller with a gloved fingertip, because a shard of glass wedged beside a vane restricts flow without stopping the pump outright. Third, check the spray arms, since E22 is about water not moving through the machine as expected: unscrew the lower arm, which usually lifts off or unclips, and hold it up to a light to see whether the jets are blocked with scale and grit. Clear them with a toothpick or a stiff wire. Fourth, if all of that is clean, move on to the drain path itself and treat it like an E24: walk the drain hose for kinks, clear the air gap on the countertop, and check the disposal connection. The cost only jumps at the point where the drain pump itself needs replacing, which is a $50 to $120 part and about an hour of work, and you should only get there after the filters, the pump cover, the impeller and the hose have all been ruled out.

Differences between models

Bosch uses the E22 to E25 block for everything on the drain and circulation side, and the exact split between them shifts across model generations and between the US and European service documentation. E22 is consistently the filter end of that range, but the same clog can show as E22 on one machine and E24 on another, so clear the filters, the pump housing and the drain hose as one job rather than treating the number as a precise diagnosis. On hidden-control models the code shows only on the inner panel display, so from outside you just see a cycle that stops.

When to stop and call someone

E22 rarely needs a technician, so call one only if you have cleaned the filters, seated the pump cover, freed the impeller and cleared the drain hose and the code keeps returning, because at that point you are looking at a pump or a sensing fault rather than a blockage. Call sooner if you find water standing in the base of the machine under the tub rather than in the tub itself, since that is a leak and a different problem. And if the machine is also throwing a drain code alongside E22, treat the drain fault as the real one and come back to the filter afterward.

Questions people ask

Where is the filter on a Bosch dishwasher?

In the floor of the tub, under the lower spray arm. You will see a cylindrical plastic filter standing in a well, sitting in a flat mesh screen that covers the rest of the sump. The cylinder turns anticlockwise a quarter turn and lifts out, and the flat screen lifts out after it. No tools are needed for either.

How often should I clean the Bosch dishwasher filter?

Once a month if the machine runs most days, every couple of months if it runs a few times a week. The job takes about five minutes at the sink. Scraping plates properly before loading matters more than pre-rinsing them, because it is soft food debris and grease that mats under the flat screen and eventually triggers the code.

Can I still use the dishwasher with an E22 code?

You can, but the wash quality will be poor because water is not circulating the way the machine expects, and the blockage gets worse with every cycle. There is no safety risk. Since the fix is a five-minute clean at the sink, running it dirty to put the job off is a false economy.

Why did E22 appear suddenly when I clean the filter regularly?

Two common reasons. The filter was refitted without being twisted and locked, which lets debris bypass it, and that gives the same code as a dirty filter. Or something hard and small, a fruit pit or a bottle label, went down into the sump in a single load and blocked the inlet under an otherwise clean screen.

What is the difference between E22 and E24 on a Bosch dishwasher?

E22 points at the filters in the sump, E24 at the rest of the drain path, so the hose, air gap and disposal connection. The two overlap enough across model generations that the same blockage can show up as either. Clean the filters first since that is quickest, then walk the drain hose if the code stays.

Will a dishwasher cleaning tablet clear E22?

Partly. A degreasing cleaner run on a hot empty cycle helps with the grease film on the mesh and is worth doing as maintenance after the repair. It will not shift a mat of soft debris, a fruit pit in the sump or a filter that is not locked down. Clean it by hand first, then use the tablet to keep it clean.
